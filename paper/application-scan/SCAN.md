# Problem-level novelty scan: thermoelastic waves and phononic structures

**Scope and date:** targeted search of primary research available up to 2 November 2026. This is an application-level screen, not an exhaustive priority search or a new constitutive model. The existing GN-III, MGT, three-phase-lag and two-phase Eringen families are to be used as published, without adding terms. A precise article, thermal law, kernel, geometry and boundary conditions must be fixed before starting any calculation. **Known** = an equivalent result for the indicated subproblem is documented; **partial** = substantial overlap but equivalence for the precise proposed problem is unresolved; **open** = no equivalent located in this targeted search, not proof that none exists. Where only an abstract or snippet was read, full equations and numerical results are **unverified**.

## P1 — Lamb/Rayleigh–Lamb waves in a nonlocal thermoelastic plate

**Verdict: PARTIAL.** Related thermoelastic/nonlocal Lamb-wave dispersion and attenuation are **known**; the exact MGT or GN-III + *two-phase* Eringen flat-plate system with a specified integral kernel, mechanical and thermal face conditions, and symmetric/antisymmetric mode set remains **unverified**, not proven new.

**Closest primary sources**
1. Xianhui Wang, Xiaoqiang Ren, Hongmei Zhou, Jiangong Yu and Ke Li, “Dynamics of thermoelastic Lamb waves in functionally graded nanoplates based on the modified nonlocal theory,” *Applied Mathematical Modelling* **117** (2023), 142–161, [10.1016/j.apm.2022.12.022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apm.2022.12.022). Publisher abstract and introduction read; **full text/results unverified**. LS thermal theory and a modified nonlocal *integral* theory already yield complex-wavenumber dispersion and attenuation. This is a direct competing guided-wave result, although not evidence of exact MGT/GN-III or two-phase equivalence. The source flags discrepancies between some integral and differential treatments.
2. Narayan Das, Soumen De and Nantu Sarkar, “Plane waves in Moore–Gibson–Thompson thermoelasticity considering nonlocal elasticity effect,” *Mathematics and Mechanics of Solids* **28**(9) (2023), 1978–1998, [10.1177/10812865221145737](https://doi.org/10.1177/10812865221145737). Author-uploaded full-text governing equations and problem formulation inspected. Existing nonlocal MGT, LS and GN-III bulk waves and free-half-space reflection, **not** a two-face Lamb plate.
3. Rakhi Tiwari and Ravi Kumar, “Analysis of plane wave propagation under the purview of three phase lag theory of thermoelasticity with non-local effect,” *European Journal of Mechanics – A/Solids* **88** (2021), 104235, [10.1016/j.euromechsol.2021.104235](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.euromechsol.2021.104235). Author-uploaded governing equations inspected. Nonlocal three-phase-lag bulk/half-space plane waves, **not** Lamb modes; the paper uses a second-order heat-flux lag approximation. It is not identical to every other three-phase-lag law.

**Potential checkable addition:** choose one homogeneous isotropic plate, one explicit strain-driven two-phase integral constitutive law, one published MGT formulation, and stress-free/insulated and stress-free/isothermal faces. Compute S/A branch complex wavenumbers at real frequency, mode shapes and attenuation versus thermal relaxation, nonlocal length and mixture fraction; assess whether thermal modes overlap mechanical branches. Such an extension may be incremental and its priority is unverified. A bounded-domain kernel and its constitutive boundary conditions cannot be inferred merely from a bulk differential equation.

**Method and deliverables:** normal-mode reduction, parity split, integral-kernel spectral/Legendre discretization, complex-root continuation and residual checks; independent discretization/root search. Deliver determinant/eigenproblem, mode classification, complex branch plots and convergence table.

**Validation required:** published elastic Rayleigh–Lamb S/A branches at zero thermoelastic coupling and local elasticity; local published thermoelastic/LS plate data with exactly matching thermal laws and face conditions; suitable GN-III limit when the chosen MGT source has it; numerical convergence and original integral-law residual at the faces. Compare Wang et al. only after matching their modified kernel, grading, LS law and boundary conditions, not by treating their model as ours.

## P2 — Band structures in a periodic nonlocal thermoelastic medium

**Verdict: PARTIAL overall; the GN-III + Eringen band-structure subproblem is KNOWN.** A blanket claim that nonlocal GN-III phononic band gaps are new is false. The exact MGT + two-phase Eringen layered longitudinal geometry, and a comparison separating attenuation mechanisms, were not established in the accessible papers and remain **unverified**.

**Closest primary source:** Seyed Mahmoud Hosseini and Chuanzeng Zhang, “Nonlocal coupled thermoelastic wave propagation band structures of nano-scale phononic crystal beams based on GN theory with energy dissipation: An analytical solution,” *Wave Motion* **92** (2020), 102429, [10.1016/j.wavemoti.2019.102429](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.wavemoti.2019.102429). Publisher abstract/introduction and available section snippets read; **full equations, kernel/boundary equivalence and numerical data unverified**. It explicitly uses GN type III, Eringen nonlocality, alternating aluminium/epoxy beam sections, interface continuity, Bloch–Floquet/transfer matrices and small-scale parameter sweeps. Its geometry is a periodic beam, not automatically the same as a longitudinal layered continuum, and the abstract does not verify a two-phase local/nonlocal mixture.

Nearby primary work: S. M. Hosseini and C. Zhang, “Band structure analysis of Green-Naghdi-based thermoelastic wave propagation in cylindrical phononic crystals with energy dissipation using a meshless collocation method,” *International Journal of Mechanical Sciences* **209** (2021), 106711; DOI and full text **unverified**. Abstract-level information found in the 2020 publisher's citing list confirms periodic cylindrical GN band calculations, not the proposed full model. Seyed Mahmoud Hosseini, “Frequency band-gaps analysis of Guyer-Krumhansl (GK) thermoelastic wave propagation in nano-sized phononic crystals: Modified GK model with energy dissipation,” *Applied Mathematical Modelling* **157** (2026), 116992: DOI and full text **unverified**; publisher's author listing describes relaxation/length-scale sweeps. GK thermal nonlocality must not be conflated with Eringen mechanical nonlocality.

**Potential checkable addition:** use a specified *existing* MGT and two-phase Eringen law for a binary periodic longitudinal structure; compare complex Bloch multipliers and finite-stack transmission with GN-III limits, varying the relaxation time, elastic length and mixture. Newness would depend on exact comparison with full papers, especially Hosseini–Zhang and other MGT searches; merely switching thermal law or plotting parameter shifts is not enough.

**Method and deliverables:** periodized integral Bloch eigenproblem; a transfer/scattering formulation only if its constitutive interface conditions are derived from that same nonlocal integral law; finite-stack frequency-response calculation. Deliver complex bands, mode shapes, attenuation maps, a finite-stack transmission spectrum and convergence/error data.

**Validation required:** reproduce an accessible published periodic GN benchmark with exactly its beam assumptions before claiming replication; uniform-medium dispersion for the exact chosen operator; zero nonlocal-length and zero thermoelastic-coupling limits against classical bilayer elastic Bloch dispersion; MGT-to-GN-III limit if supported by the chosen reference; a homogeneous-cell no-Bragg-gap check. Compute power flux/energy balance for the stated model. In a dissipative medium a real-frequency complex Bloch wave may attenuate even in a pass band: report operational thresholds and separate periodic scattering from thermal absorption using controlled comparisons, not an automatic zero/nonzero imaginary-wavenumber definition of a strict band gap.

## P3 — Interfaces, reflection/transmission and surface waves

**Verdict: PARTIAL overall; free-surface reflection under nonlocal MGT and GN-III and nonlocal GN-III layer/half-space Rayleigh waves are KNOWN.** Whether an identical *two-phase* Eringen + MGT transmission problem for two distinct media has appeared is **unverified**. Do not market surface waves or free-surface reflection in these models as a new generic problem.

**Closest primary sources:**
* Das, De and Sarkar (2023), citation in P1: inspected governing equations and reflection formulation. Nonlocal MGT reflected plane waves at a stress-free isothermal/insulated half-space; no claim of a two-medium transmission study.
* Siddhartha Biswas, “Rayleigh waves in a nonlocal thermoelastic layer lying over a nonlocal thermoelastic half-space,” *Acta Mechanica* **231** (2020), 4129–4144, [10.1007/s00707-020-02751-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00707-020-02751-2). Publisher abstract read, **full equations unverified**. Explicit Eringen + GN-III layer/half-space surface-wave dispersion, attenuation and penetration are already reported.
* Xianhui Wang, Yaohui Chen, Jiangong Yu, Yanwei Liu, Bo Zhang and Hongliang Zhou, “Reflection, transmission, and dissipation of plane waves in sandwiched functionally graded thermo-electro-elastic nanoplates via nonlocal integral elasticity theory,” *Composite Structures* **324** (2023), 117564, [10.1016/j.compstruct.2023.117564](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compstruct.2023.117564). Publisher abstract/introduction inspected; **full model and results unverified**. LS and modified nonlocal integral elasticity with sandwiched geometry already address reflection, transmission and dissipation, though not identical to two ordinary MGT half-spaces.
* Tuan T. Tran, Dung T. T. Truong, Anh T. N. Vu and Vinh C. Pham, “Weakly nonlocal thermoelasticity of type II: Rayleigh waves in coated half-spaces,” *Journal of Applied Physics* **140** (2026), 064302, [10.1063/5.0334224](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0334224). Abstract only; **full proof unverified**. Reports nonlocal constitutive-boundary incompatibility for a class of layered problems; its alternative weakly nonlocal theory is not automatically equivalent to Eringen two-phase. This is a material feasibility warning, not a proof that every two-phase model fails.

**Potential checkable addition:** a two-material interface with a *published* two-phase strain-driven kernel and published MGT law in both media, comparing mechanically perfect and explicitly specified thermally perfect contact, with incoming/outgoing mode amplitudes and power balance. This exact specialization requires a full prior-art search and a defensible nonlocal cross-interface kernel; no assumption that local continuity alone closes it.

**Method/deliverables:** complex wavenumber mode decomposition plus scattering or a direct integral numerical method; specify outgoing/decaying conditions and constitutive interface conditions. Deliver mode conversion and phase-resolved flux/transmission curves with uncertainty/residual checks.

**Validation required:** no contrast/no interface reflection, classical local elastodynamic scattering at zero coupling, local thermoelastic benchmark with matched thermal conditions, reciprocity and the correct coupled time-averaged power balance including dissipation and any interpoint energy flux. Classical R+T=1 is not generally valid for a lossy medium unless absorption and measurement surfaces are accounted for.

## P4 — A finite phononic crystal with a single defect: thermoelastic resonance and loss

**Verdict: PARTIAL.** Ordinary defect states and nonlocal size effects in phononic systems are already **known**; a single-defect, finite, two-phase Eringen + MGT thermoelastic **longitudinal** stack with documented resonance linewidth and thermal/mechanical energy accounting was not located in this targeted search. Its exact priority is **unverified**; it is an application question, not a new model or claim of a first defect mode.

**Primary comparison:** Jun Jin, Ningdong Hu and Hongping Hu, “Size effects on the mixed modes and defect modes for a nano-scale phononic crystal slab,” *Applied Mathematics and Mechanics* **44** (2023), 21–34, [10.1007/s10483-023-2945-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10483-023-2945-6). Publisher abstract inspected, **full equations/results unverified**. It already studies a defect in a phononic supercell with nonlocal strain-gradient elasticity and size-dependent defect modes. It is a three-dimensional slab and does not establish the requested MGT/two-phase thermoelastic calculation. Hosseini–Zhang (P2) already treats nonlocal GN-III periodic bands without the specified finite single-defect resonance; the full text must be checked for any defect treatment before claiming this difference. Other prior thermoelastic defect-resonance work may exist.

**Specific, checkable question:** In a finite A/B periodic rod with a single prescribed thickness defect, does a resonance inside a reference elastic stop-band remain spatially localized when a *published* MGT heat law and a *published* two-phase nonlocal elastic law are applied, and how do its frequency, linewidth, localization and transmitted mechanical power change with thermal relaxation and nonlocal length? Use one fixed illustrative material pair and one defect geometry. Report both complex poles when the relevant boundary-value problem supports them and measurable real-frequency peaks; do not assert a pole or high-Q resonance will exist in advance. With dissipation, define the frequency window using the lossless/local reference and state a transmission threshold, not an exact gap without qualification. Separate nonlocal mechanical interactions from thermal loss by specified counterfactual runs; do not subtract attenuation coefficients and call the result unique heat production.

**Method and expected deliverables:** formulate no new constitutive law: choose the published equations and their matching energy/interface conventions, then apply them to a finite defect stack. Use a direct integral discretization across all interfaces and the finite exterior conditions; independently check against a mathematically equivalent formulation only after proving the required constitutive and boundary conditions. A standard layer transfer matrix is not automatically valid for a kernel spanning several layers. Compute the spatially resolved displacement/temperature profiles, energy/flux budget, complex resonant frequency where well-defined, real-frequency transmission, peak width and convergence as cell count, grid/order and kernel quadrature vary. A transmission peak need not be a localized eigenmode: verify spatial localization and pole/finite-size behavior separately.

**Validation required:** (i) remove the defect and recover the reference periodic finite-stack response; (ii) local elastic uncoupled limit against a published classical layered-defect solution or independently calculated transfer-matrix benchmark; (iii) reproduce a published periodic GN/nonlocal benchmark only after matching the exact beam/rod model; (iv) independent finite-domain integral versus legitimately equivalent differential checks including constitutive boundary/interface conditions; (v) recover homogeneous elastic/thermoelastic dispersion; (vi) mesh, integration, sample-size and source-frequency convergence; (vii) compare the MGT→GN-III limit and zero coupling using the actual published constitutive equations; and (viii) model-specific power balance and passivity check, including interpoint energy exchange if necessary. No experiment or measured-material validation is presumed. A complex pole's Q=Re(ω)/(−2 Im(ω)) applies only under the stated e^−iωt convention and weakly damped isolated resonance; peak-width Q need not coincide when peaks overlap or are asymmetric.

## One recommendation

**Recommend P4: the finite, single-defect periodic rod and its thermoelastic resonance/energy budget.** It uses the author's periodic-wave expertise, avoids duplicating the planned smart-plate work in Lamb, and asks a sharper physical question than repeating nonlocal GN-III band diagrams. It is only a *conditional application opportunity*: generic defect modes, nonlocal size effects and thermoelastic phononic bands are already known, and full-text priority and model-specific interface feasibility are gating checks. No novelty or publishability is certified.

7. Experimental applicability, material calibration and independently reviewed priority or proof. None is established by this scan.
