## 2. Detailed Analysis of Prior Art on Nonlocal Thermodynamics, Kernel Symmetry, and Boundary Conditions

### 2.1 Edelen & Laws (1971) and Eringen (1974): Integral Nonlocal Thermoelasticity
In **Eringen (1974)** (*"Theory of nonlocal thermoelasticity"*, *Int. J. Eng. Sci.* 12:1063–1077, verified in full text):
- Eringen enforces the local balance laws holding only after integration over the body $\mathcal{B}$, introducing the **localization residuals** $\hat{f}_k$ (mass), $\hat{p}_k$ (momentum), $\hat{l}_{kl}$ (moment of momentum), and $\hat{\omega}$ (energy localization residual) subject to zero body integrals:
  $$\int_{\mathcal{B}} \hat{\omega}\,dv = 0 \qquad (\text{Eringen 1974, Eq. 2.13}).$$
- From the Clausius–Duhem inequality with helmholtz free energy $\psi$, Eringen derives the **exact linear nonlocal thermoelastic constitutive equations** in Section 4, Eqs. (4.8)–(4.9):
  $$t_{kl}(\mathbf{x}, t) = \int_V \left[\lambda'(|x-x'|)e_{rr}(x')\,\delta_{kl} + 2\mu'(|x-x'|)e_{kl}(x') - \beta'(|x-x'|)T(x')\delta_{kl}\right]dv(x'), \qquad (\text{Eq. 4.8})$$
  $$\rho\eta(\mathbf{x}, t) = \rho\eta_0 + \frac{\rho\gamma_0}{T_0}T(\mathbf{x}, t) + \int_V \alpha'(|x-x'|)T(x')\,dv(x') + \int_V \beta'(|x-x'|)e_{kk}(x')\,dv(x'). \qquad (\text{Eq. 4.9})$$
- **Crucial priority conclusion**: **Eringen (1974), Eqs. (4.8)–(4.9) already proved that the nonlocal attenuation kernel $\beta'(|x-x'|)$ multiplying temperature $T(x')$ in the stress tensor $t_{kl}$ MUST be identical to the nonlocal attenuation kernel $\beta'(|x-x'|)$ multiplying dilatation $e_{kk}(x')$ in the entropy density $\rho\eta$!**
  - If $\beta'(|x-x'|) = \beta\,\mathcal{K}_{\gamma,\ell}(x, x')$ (nonlocal thermal stress), then $\rho\eta$ **must** contain $\beta\mathcal{L}_{\gamma,\ell}[\varepsilon_{kk}]$ (**Variant V1**).
  - If $\beta'(|x-x'|) = \beta\,\delta(x-x')$ (local thermal stress, while $\lambda', \mu'$ carry the nonlocal kernel), then $\rho\eta$ **must** contain the local dilational entropy $\beta\varepsilon_{kk}$ (**Variant V2**).
  - Therefore, the general thermodynamic duality underlying **Theorem 1** is **not new**: it is the direct specialization of Eringen (1974), Eqs. (4.8)–(4.9) (and Polizzotto's 2001/2003 self-adjoint operator formalism) to the two-phase local/nonlocal operator $\mathcal{L}_{\gamma,\ell}$.

### 2.2 Polizzotto (2001, 2003a, 2003b) and Borino, Failla & Parrinello (2003): Nonlocality Residual and Normalized Symmetric Kernel
- **Polizzotto (2001, 2003a)** (verified via Marotti de Sciarra 2009, Eqs. 3–19): Formulates the thermodynamics of nonlocal materials using a **nonlocality energy residual** $P(\mathbf{x})$ representing the power exchanged by long-range intermolecular interactions, subject to the insulation condition
  $$\int_\Omega P(\mathbf{x})\,d\mathbf{x} = 0.$$
  Writing the global free energy as a quadratic form involving a linear integral regularization operator $\mathcal{R}$, Polizzotto proves that $\int_\Omega P(\mathbf{x})\,d\mathbf{x} = 0$ holds for arbitrary strain/temperature rates **if and only if** the nonlocal operator $\mathcal{R}$ is **self-adjoint on $L^2(\Omega)$** ($\mathcal{R}(x, y) = \mathcal{R}(y, x)$).
- **Borino, Failla & Parrinello (2003)** (verified via Marotti de Sciarra 2009, Eq. 5 and Di Luzio 2005, Eq. 5): Recognizes that an unnormalized attenuation kernel $K_\ell(x, y)$ on a bounded domain $\Omega$ fails to reproduce constant fields ($\int_\Omega K_\ell(x,y)\,dy = w_\Omega(x) < 1$ near $\partial\Omega$), while multiplicative renormalization $K_\ell(x,y)/w_\Omega(x)$ destroys symmetry and thus violates Polizzotto's thermodynamic self-adjointness axiom. They introduce the **symmetric, constant-preserving generalized attenuation function**:
  $$W_\ell^{\text{BFP}}(x, y) = \left(1 - w_\Omega(x)\right)\delta(x - y) + K_\ell(x, y), \qquad \int_\Omega W_\ell^{\text{BFP}}(x, y)\,dy \equiv 1, \qquad W_\ell^{\text{BFP}}(x, y) = W_\ell^{\text{BFP}}(y, x).$$
  As we proved in Task 1 (`artifact_test.md`), using $W_\ell^{\text{BFP}}$ in Variant V2 eliminates the static $1/\sqrt{\gamma}$ thermal-expansion boundary layer while preserving every bulk and self-adjointness property of Theorem 1.

### 2.3 Polizzotto (2014, 2015, 2016) and Yu, Tian & Liu (2015) / Yu, Tian & Xiong (2016): Stress-Gradient Formulations and Extended Irreversible Thermodynamics (EIT)
A natural question is: *Can the "Camp 1" equation pair — $(1 - \ell^2\partial_x^2)\sigma = E\varepsilon - \beta\theta$ paired with $\rho\eta = \beta\varepsilon + \frac{\rho c_E}{T_0}\theta$ — be rescued by viewing $(1 - \ell^2\partial_x^2)\sigma = E\varepsilon - \beta\theta$ not as an Eringen integral model in $(\varepsilon, \theta)$, but as a Polizzotto (2014, 2015, 2016) stress-gradient model in $(\sigma, \nabla\sigma, \theta)$ or an Extended Irreversible Thermodynamics (EIT) model as in Yu, Tian & Liu (2015) and Yu, Tian & Xiong (2016)?*

We test this explicitly (proved symbolically in `artifact_test.py`, `verify_symbolic_artifact_and_survivors`):
1. **Complementary Free Enthalpy / Gibbs Potential $g(\sigma, \sigma_{,x}, \theta)$ (Polizzotto 2014, 2015, 2016)**:
   In Polizzotto's stress-gradient framework, the independent state variables are $(\sigma, \sigma_{,x}, \theta)$ with complementary free-enthalpy density
   $$g(\sigma, \sigma_{,x}, \theta) = \frac{1}{2E}\sigma^2 + \frac{\ell^2}{2E}(\sigma_{,x})^2 + \frac{\beta}{E}\sigma\theta + \frac{\rho c_\sigma}{2T_0}\theta^2,$$
   where $\frac{\rho c_\sigma}{T_0} = \frac{\rho c_E}{T_0} + \frac{\beta^2}{E}$ is the specific heat at constant stress. Taking the variational derivative of $\int_\Omega g\,dx$ with respect to $\sigma$ (under natural boundary condition $\sigma_{,x} = 0$ on $\partial\Omega$) yields the strain state law:
   $$\varepsilon = \frac{\delta G}{\delta \sigma} = \frac{1}{E}\left(\sigma - \ell^2\sigma_{,xx}\right) + \frac{\beta}{E}\theta \iff (1 - \ell^2\partial_x^2)\sigma = E\varepsilon - \beta\theta \quad (\text{Form A!}).$$
   **However**, taking the conjugate partial derivative with respect to $\theta$ gives the mandatory Gibbs entropy density:
   $$\rho\eta^{\text{Gibbs}} = \frac{\partial g}{\partial \theta} = \frac{\beta}{E}\sigma + \frac{\rho c_\sigma}{T_0}\theta.$$
   Now substitute $\sigma = E\varepsilon - \beta\theta + \ell^2\sigma_{,xx}$ into $\rho\eta^{\text{Gibbs}}$:
   $$\rho\eta^{\text{Gibbs}} = \frac{\beta}{E}\left(E\varepsilon - \beta\theta + \ell^2\sigma_{,xx}\right) + \left(\frac{\rho c_E}{T_0} + \frac{\beta^2}{E}\right)\theta = \underbrace{\beta\varepsilon + \frac{\rho c_E}{T_0}\theta}_{\rho\eta^{\text{loc}}\text{ (Camp 1)}} + \underbrace{\frac{\beta\ell^2}{E}\sigma_{,xx}}_{\text{non-zero stress-Laplacian term!}}.$$
   **Result**: Even in Polizzotto's stress-gradient Gibbs representation, $(1 - \ell^2\partial_x^2)\sigma = E\varepsilon - \beta\theta$ is **thermodynamically incompatible** with the local constant-strain entropy $\rho\eta^{\text{loc}} = \beta\varepsilon + \frac{\rho c_E}{T_0}\theta$ unless the missing stress-Laplacian term $\frac{\beta\ell^2}{E}\sigma_{,xx}$ is added to the entropy (or equivalently unless $\beta\varepsilon$ is replaced by $\frac{\beta}{E}\sigma$ and $c_E$ by $c_\sigma$)!
2. **Yu, Tian & Liu (2015) and Yu, Tian & Xiong (2016)** (*Eur. J. Mech. A/Solids* 51:96–106; 60:238–253):
   - In Yu, Tian & Liu (2015) and Yu, Tian & Xiong (2016) (the latter verified from ScienceDirect preview and secondary equations in the Drive corpus), the authors attempt to justify $(1 - \ell^2\nabla^2)\sigma_{ij} = \sigma_{ij}^L = C_{ijkl}\varepsilon_{kl} - \beta_{ij}\theta$ alongside a Guyer–Krumhansl / nonlocal heat conduction equation $(1 - \ell_q^2\nabla^2 + \tau_0\partial_t)\mathbf{q} = -\kappa\nabla\theta$ while retaining the local constant-strain entropy $\rho\eta = \beta_{ij}\varepsilon_{ij} + \frac{\rho c_E}{T_0}\theta$ in the energy equation $\rho T_0\dot{\eta} = -q_{i,i}$.
   - As proved above in both Helmholtz $(\varepsilon, \theta)$ and Gibbs $(\sigma, \theta)$ representations, retaining $\rho\eta = \beta\varepsilon_{kk} + \frac{\rho c_E}{T_0}\theta$ alongside $(1 - \ell^2\nabla^2)\sigma_{ij} = \lambda\varepsilon_{kk}\delta_{ij} + 2\mu\varepsilon_{ij} - \beta\theta\delta_{ij}$ leaves an uncompensated mechanical-thermal cross-power $\int_\Omega (\sigma_{ij}\dot{\varepsilon}_{ij} - \rho\eta\dot{\theta} - \dot{\psi})\,dV \neq 0$ that cannot be absorbed into any divergence-free extra entropy flux for general independent $(\varepsilon, \theta)$ fields.

### 2.4 Giorgi & Morro (2025) (*Thermo*, Full Text Verified)
In **Giorgi & Morro (2025)** (*"Thermodynamics and nonlocality in continuum physics"*, *Thermo*, full text read from `iris.unibs.it`):
- The authors formulate **weakly nonlocal (gradient) continuum thermodynamics** using the Clausius–Duhem inequality with an extra entropy flux vector $\mathbf{k}$ ($\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{q}/\theta + \mathbf{k})$) and variational derivatives $\delta_\phi^{(2)}\psi = \partial_\phi\psi - \nabla\cdot\partial_{\nabla\phi}\psi + \nabla\cdot(\nabla\cdot\partial_{\nabla\nabla\phi}\psi)$.
- In Section 3.1 (*"Thermoelasticity with nonlocality of the strain"*), they consider a strain-gradient free energy $\psi(\theta, \mathbf{E}, \nabla\mathbf{E}, \nabla\nabla\mathbf{E})$ and prove (Eqs. 19–20) that:
  $$\eta = -\delta_\theta^{(2)}\psi, \qquad \mathbf{T}_{RR} = \rho_R\,\delta_{\mathbf{E}}^{(2)}\psi.$$
  Consequently, whenever the strain-gradient coefficients in $\psi$ depend on temperature $\theta$, the entropy $\eta$ **must** include the corresponding strain-gradient terms; conversely, if the thermal coupling in $\psi$ is purely local ($\psi$ contains no $\theta$ dependence in the $\nabla\mathbf{E}$ terms), then the thermal stress in $\mathbf{T}_{RR}$ is purely local and $\eta$ is purely local.
- **What Giorgi & Morro (2025) do NOT cover**: They do not study Eringen integral operators, two-phase kernels $\mathcal{L}_{\gamma,\ell}$, the differential Form A shortcut $(1 - \ell^2\nabla^2)\sigma = \sigma^{\text{loc}}$, Constitutive Boundary Conditions on bounded domains, or thermoelastic wave dispersion/mode counting.

### 2.5 Nobili & Pramanik (2025a, 2025b) (*Mech. Time-Depend. Mater.* & *Int. J. Eng. Sci.*, Full Texts Verified)
In **Nobili & Pramanik (2025b)** (*"A well-posed theory of linear non-local elasticity"*, *Int. J. Eng. Sci.* 215:104314, Oct 2025, full text read):
- The authors analyze **isothermal** 1D nonlocal Euler–Bernoulli beams.
- In Section 2.1 (Eqs. 10–13), they review the **Two-Phase Nonlocal Model (TPNM)** $\mathcal{R}_2[\chi] = \xi_1\chi + \xi_2 \mathcal{K}*\chi = D^{-1}M$ ($\xi_1 = \gamma, \xi_2 = 1-\gamma$), write the exact 2nd-order ODE $\chi - \xi_1\epsilon^2\chi'' = D^{-1}(M - \epsilon^2 M'')$ (Eq. 12), and write the exact Robin CBCs $\chi'(a) - \frac{1}{\epsilon}\chi(a) = (\xi_1 D)^{-1}(M'(a) - \frac{1}{\epsilon}M(a))$ (Eq. 13), crediting the isothermal TPNM beam ODE/CBCs to **Challamel & Wang (2008)**, **Fernández-Sáez et al. (2016)**, **Romano et al. (2017)**, and **Mikhasev & Nobili (2020)**.
- In Section 2.2 (Eqs. 14–16), they analyze **modified-kernel nonlocal elasticity** (including reflecting/generalized boundary kernels) and show it leads to generalized Robin CBCs.
- Critically, they remark in Section 2.1 that TPNM's volume fractions $\xi_1, \xi_2$ lack direct experimental calibration.
- **What Nobili & Pramanik (2025a, 2025b) do NOT cover**: Both papers are **strictly isothermal** ($\theta \equiv 0$). Neither paper considers thermoelastic coupling, entropy, the V1 vs. V2 distinction, the Camp 1 Clausius–Duhem violation, or thermoelastic wave dispersion.
