## 2. Complete Inventory of Author-Stated Open Problems (Sec. 11.1, pp. 92–95)

### A. Theory (pp. 92–93)
1. **Gap 1 — Analytical classification theorem for the 3-lag template (p. 92):** Complete analytical conditions on $(\tau_q, \tau_T, \tau_v, k, k^*)$ and coupling $\varepsilon > 0$ governing well-posedness, decay, and front speeds for 1st-order DPL, TPL, and MGT (including an analytical proof of why 1st-order DPL exhibits no unstable roots for positive lags).
2. **Gap 2 — Thermodynamics of truncated lag models (p. 92):** Systematic construction of energy functionals and entropy inequalities across Taylor truncation orders of DPL and TPL.
3. **Gap 3 — Memory-dependent (MDD) and fractional models (pp. 92–93):** Energy functionals, thermodynamic admissibility, uniqueness, and classification of MDD kernels by large-$s$ behavior of $s\kappa(s)$.
4. **Gap 4 — Interface conditions for lagged and memory models (p. 93):** Thermodynamically consistent imperfect/Kapitza interface conditions, including interfacial entropy production, for lagged and memory heat laws.
5. **Gap 5 — Mode interference in dissipative media (p. 93):** Systematic energy-flux apportionment separating bulk cross-mode interference from interface dissipation in lossy media.

### B. Combined Size & Memory Effects (p. 93)
6. **Gap 6 — Joint size and memory validation against lattice dynamics (p. 93):** Reference lattice/atomistic benchmark with heat carriers to discriminate among heuristic nonlocal/gradient + hyperbolic/memory combinations.
7. **Gap 7 — Calibration protocols for thermoelastic length scales (p. 93):** Resolving the $\sim 33\%$ discrepancy between Brillouin-zone-boundary ($\xi \approx 0.386a$) and long-wavelength ($\xi = a/\sqrt{12} \approx 0.289a$) calibrations in coupled thermoelasticity.
8. **Gap 8 — Thermodynamically consistent nonlocal thermoelastic coupling (p. 93):** Resolving Form A (unsmoothed thermal stress) vs. Form B (smoothed thermal stress) near the cutoff by deriving the coupling directly from a nonlocal Helmholtz free energy rather than choosing between (54) and (55) heuristically.
9. **Gap 9 — Nanoscale surface and interface thermoelasticity (p. 93):** Incorporating Gurtin–Murdoch surface stress, surface heat capacity, and Kapitza resistance into thermoelastic surface-wave and interface-partition models.

### C. Experiments, Data & Computation (p. 93)
10. **Gap 10 — Simultaneous measurement of elastic-type and thermal-type branches in one solid across $\omega^*$ (p. 93).**
11. **Gap 11 — Independent (non-fitted) determination of phase lags combining pump–probe and ultrasonic attenuation (p. 93).**
12. **Gap 12 — Systematic GHz resonator thermoelastic damping datasets vs. size and frequency (p. 93).**
13. **Gap 13 — Shared high-precision numerical benchmark suite for heat pulse, thermal shock, Rayleigh wave, and interface partition (p. 93).**
14. **Gap 14 — Fast $O(N\log N)$ / sum-of-exponentials time-domain memory & fractional operators with rigorous error bounds (p. 93).**

### D. Ten Decisive Evidentiary Questions (`R1`–`R10`, Table 17, p. 95)
- **`R1`:** Exact time-domain total vs. availability energy flux per generalized family under explicit free-energy and BC conventions.
- **`R2`:** Exact interface energy balance in generalized and nonlocal media tested against a model-independent reference solution.
- **`R3`:** Model-independent reference benchmarks (exact analytic limit, symbolic determinant, or independent discretization).
- **`R4`–`R5`, `R7`–`R10`:** Experimental branch observability, observable-based flux normalization, parameter identifiability, anisotropic thermodynamic admissibility, convention-respecting model comparisons, and shared convergence benchmarks.
