## Ivanov–Pandolfi (2009), original published PDF, item 1

Inspected pp. 1–6 (including Definition 1, equations (12)–(21) and Theorem 2), with targeted sections through conclusion. This is substantially stronger evidence than the earlier secondary abstract. Their equation (1) has memory applied to `theta_xx`, and three control settings: one boundary, a fixed distributed profile, and distributed subdomain control. Definition 1 asks, for each initial vector, for an eventually vanishing control and eventually vanishing temperature. Equations (14)–(19) reduce rest to interpolation for the Laplace transform of a compactly supported control at **each** root of `lambda+n^2 G(lambda)=0`, with interpolation data determined by the initial coefficients. Their Theorem 2 uses a zero of G and roots accumulating at that zero to obstruct controllability to rest (for their specified boundary/profile settings). The analytic accumulation argument is explicit on p. 6. This is a different target and equation from the zero-start, *single-time temperature-only* null space in the present paper. Nevertheless there is a genuine structural overlap: accumulated spectral points and entire Laplace interpolation are central to both. Difference of targets is not a proof of novelty or absence of an equivalent intermediate result. Exact equivalence of the two characteristic equations under possible state transformations still needs a careful full proof audit.

## Fattorini–Russell (1974), original published PDF, item 3

Inspected pp. 45–49, especially Theorem 1.2, Corollary 1.4 and Theorem 1.5, plus the introduction. Their basis is `exp(-lambda_n t)` and Theorem 1.5(b) gives an `L²(0,ell)` biorthogonal sequence with norm `<= B exp(K G(lambda_n))`, under Theorem 1.2's conditions on an increasing convex F and inverse G, and spacing `G(lambda_1)>l`, `G(lambda_{n+1})-G(lambda_n)>l`. For our strictly positive sequence with uniform positive square-root gap, set `F(v)=v²`, `G(v)=sqrt(v)`, and choose `l < min(sqrt(b_1), inf_n(sqrt(b_{n+1})-sqrt(b_n))`. F is increasing and convex, `integral_1^infinity 1/F(v) dv` is finite and `G(uv)=G(u)G(v)`. Thus the theorem supplies real negative-exponential biorthogonals with `||q_n|| <= B exp(K sqrt(b_n))` for fixed `ell` and fixed gap data. These are exactly the negative-exponential family required *after reversal* in the construction; no reversal is then needed. On this reading, **the manuscript's qualitative fixed-parameter smooth right-inverse construction can use this classical 1974 result in place of the CMV preprint**, subject to checking the printed symbols against the scan and independent review. This does not supply CMV's explicit two-gap short-time constants, nor prove control-cost estimates for the full coupled operator. It does not establish novelty of the kernel theorem.

## Additional targeted published/accepted-source comparisons

- Item 5, Ammar Khodja et al. (2014), pp. 2084–2089: their condition (2.2) requires distinct complex `lambda_k`, `Re(lambda_k) >= delta |lambda_k| > 0`, and `sum 1/|lambda_k| < infinity`. Their Theorem 2.5 additionally requires an admissible control operator and the approximate-controllability condition (2.10); it gives null controllability for `T>T0` and failure for `T<T0`. The present full two-branch spectrum contains a bounded accumulating slow subsequence, so the reciprocal summability in (2.2) fails for that full spectrum. This does not rule out a different reformulation, nor does it transfer the minimal-time result. Fast-only rates satisfy summability, but fast-only analysis is not the coupled problem.
- Item 6, Ammar-Khodja et al. (2011), pp. 559–560: Theorem 1.1 is a Kalman-rank characterization for their stated parabolic system; Theorem 1.2 assumes complex spectral separation `|Lambda_k-Lambda_l| >= rho |k-l|` and summable reciprocal moduli for its matrix-exponential biorthogonals. Neither assumption applies directly to the entire present fast-plus-accumulating-slow family. Theorem 1.1 is not imported to the frozen heat–memory problem.
- Item 9 (accepted manuscript), Halanay–Pandolfi (2015), pp. 4–6: Theorem 2 concerns approximate controllability in the paper's temperature target space; Theorem 3 assumes the resolvent `R(T) != 0` and states failure of null controllability for some initial data in its specified systems. Their definition of admissible boundary controls requires a meaningful terminal trace. These are not a characterization of the zero-temperature memory-state set here, and approximate controllability of temperature does not imply density of that memory set.
- Item 7, Tenenbaum–Tucsnak (2007), Theorems 3.1 and 3.4: fast-control estimates for regular spectra and the specified abstract system, under their control-coefficient assumptions. A fast separated sequence is not the present coupled slow-fast sequence; no control-cost estimate is imported. The missing 2011 Tenenbaum–Tucsnak paper remains missing.
- Items 10 and 14: the supplied published papers' theorems distinguish failure of null controllability for some nonzero initial conditions from the present zero-start kernel question; the latter is not an exact controllability-to-rest assertion. Full proof-level comparisons remain a research task.
