Theorem 1.2 assumes a negative self-adjoint generator with an orthonormal eigenbasis, eigenvalues `-lambda_k` with nondecreasing positive `lambda_k -> infinity`, input operator `B:U -> X_{-beta}`, and an explicit finite-spectrum observation inequality (1.7) with exponent `gamma in (0,1)`. It concludes null controllability for every initial state and all positive times and gives a control-cost upper bound. Its proof uses a spectral inequality, an observability estimate, and a Hilbert-space factorization to solve damped moments; Proposition 2.4 provides a bounded control-producing operator. The full frozen heat–memory block system has a bounded accumulating slow branch, whereas this theorem assumes eigenvalues escaping to infinity, and its self-adjointness/orthonormal-basis and inequality conditions are not established for that system. Therefore it cannot be applied to the full system from these facts. Nor is its cost bound a bound for the correction-to-memory inverse.
