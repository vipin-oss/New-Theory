# Reachability and topology

On a fixed positive gap, Cycle 10 establishes bounded \(\mathcal A,\mathcal K:L^2(-T,-\epsilon)\to H_o\), dense unconstrained memory range using the full signed synthesis, and onto projection to each finite \(V_N\). The new flat theorem strengthens its finite-sine exclusion to smooth no-gap flat controls. It also shows that imposing exact odd temperature nulling in this flat class gives only zero odd memory. These facts do not imply full \(L^2\) no-gap controllability, \(H^2\)-density, exact reachability, or physical nonlinear realizability.

For Hilbert control space \(U\), bounded \(\mathcal A:U\to H\), \(\mathcal K:U\to H_o\), the exact annihilator is \((\mathcal K\ker\mathcal A)^\perp=\{h:\mathcal K^*h\in\overline{\operatorname{ran}\mathcal A^*}^{\,U}\}\). The overline is the \(U\)-norm closure. This identity alone does not provide a bounded separator or determine a range. The smooth flat class is not itself a closed Hilbert control space, and the flat theorem must not be extended by completion to nonflat controls.

The Cycle 10 \(\mathcal K\) is Hilbert–Schmidt on a fixed gap: the time-space kernel coefficients are \(O(1/q_n)\) uniformly there, with square-summable squares. Consequently a bounded sequence of gap controls whose temperature tends to zero and whose memory tends to a nonzero \(h\) cannot exist: weak compactness gives a subsequence \(a_j\rightharpoonup a\); compact \(\mathcal K\) gives \(\mathcal Ka=h\), boundedness gives \(\mathcal Aa=0\), and the gap theorem forces \(a=0\), contradiction. Thus any such successful approximation must have unbounded \(L^2\) control norm. This is an abstract exact consequence for fixed-gap **linearized odd** controls, not a proof that such approximations exist. Finite-mode exact controls cannot be promoted to a bounded infinite-dimensional limit without this compactness check.

## Dense joint temperature–memory range on a fixed terminal gap

**Theorem (linearized odd model).** Let \(U=L^2(-T,-\epsilon)\), \(0<\epsilon<T\), and \(J=(\mathcal A,\mathcal K):U\to H_o\times H_o\). With the physical positive constants and nonzero coupling, \(\overline{\operatorname{ran}J}=H_o\times H_o\). In particular, for any odd \(h\in H_o\) and \(\rho>0\), a smooth compactly supported boundary history can make \(\|Y(0)\|_2<\rho\) and \(\|P(0)-h\|_2<\rho\) simultaneously. If \(h\ne0\), every such approximating sequence has unbounded \(L^2\) control norm. This is approximate **linearized** preparation; exact temperature nulling and nonlinear physical realization do not follow.

**Proof.** On the fixed gap both maps are Hilbert–Schmidt: the thermal kernel's fast part has \(O(q_n e^{-c q_n^2\epsilon})\) and its slow part \(O(q_n^{-3})\); the memory kernel is \(O(q_n^{-1})\). For \((\eta,h)\in H_o^2\), the adjoint series on positive time is
\[J^*(\eta,h)(-t)=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{C_n}{\Delta_n}\bigl[(\delta_n\eta_n+\gamma h_n)e^{\lambda_{n,+}t}+(b_n\eta_n-\gamma h_n)e^{\lambda_{n,-}t}\bigr].\tag{1}\]
The slow coefficients are bounded by \(C(|\eta_n|q_n^{-3}+|h_n|q_n^{-1})\), hence absolutely summable. The fast part converges absolutely and locally uniformly for \(\Re t>0\) by Cauchy–Schwarz and Gaussian decay. Therefore the sum is holomorphic there. If (1) vanishes a.e. on \((\epsilon,T)\), analyticity makes it vanish for all positive \(t\).

For \(\Re z>0\), termwise Laplace integration is justified: \(\sum |\eta_n|q_n/|\lambda_{n,-}|<\infty\), and the slow coefficients are summable. On compact sets avoiding the two spectra and their only finite accumulation point \(-\mu\), the resulting partial fractions converge normally: slow terms use the summable \(q_n^{-3},q_n^{-1}\) coefficients and fast terms gain the divisor \(O(q_n^{-2})\). Analytic continuation in the connected complement makes the meromorphic sum zero. Both eigenvalue branches are strictly decreasing, their images lie on opposite sides of \(-\mu\), and each individual pole is isolated. The residues at the two poles of mode n yield
\[\delta_n\eta_n+\gamma h_n=0,\qquad b_n\eta_n-\gamma h_n=0.\]
Adding gives \(\Delta_n\eta_n=0\), then \(h_n=0\) since \(\gamma\ne0\). Therefore \(\ker J^*=\{0\}\) and the joint range is dense. Smooth compactly supported inputs are dense in \(U\), and boundedness of \(J\) gives the same closure for them. The unbounded-norm conclusion is the weak compactness argument above, because exact \(\mathcal A a=0\) forces \(a=0\) on the gap. ∎

This proof uses distinct isolated poles and absolute convergence, not finite-mode rank, a global ordering of the accumulating spectrum, or an observability inequality. It proves neither a prescribed \(H^2\) approximation nor nonlinear small-control feasibility. The control norm blow-up is precisely why a nonlinear small-data implicit argument cannot be inferred from linear joint density.
