# Nonlinear endpoint map and singularity

A controlled quasilinear well-posedness and Fréchet differentiability theorem is not established in this investigation. The following is the exact **conditional** second-variation equation, provided the solution map is twice differentiable in strong spaces making all products and traces meaningful. Let \((Y_i,P_i,V_i)\) be first variations in directions \((m_i,\tau_{s,i},\tau_{a,i})\), \(W_i=m_i-V_i-\alpha P_i+\beta QY_i\). For the mixed derivative \((Y_{12},P_{12},V_{12})\), the boundary and initial data are zero and
\[\mathcal M(Y_{12})_s-\kappa(Y_{12})_{xx}+b\theta_*r(P_{12})_s= -d[Y_1Q(Y_2)_s+Y_2Q(Y_1)_s]
-b[Y_1(m_2'+r(P_2)_s)+Y_2(m_1'+r(P_1)_s)]+2k\lambda W_1W_2,\]
\[(P_{12})_s=-\mu P_{12}+\gamma QY_{12},\qquad (V_{12})_s=-\lambda V_{12}.\]
No manufactured distributed source is introduced: the right side is the derivative of existing constitutive terms. Odd×odd products generate even forcing; even×odd can generate odd forcing. This is a formal mode-mixing calculation, not construction of a singular branch.

Approaches considered: an ordinary implicit-function theorem requires a bounded right inverse for the full infinite-dimensional temperature derivative, which has not been established; Lyapunov–Schmidt requires a closed complemented range and finite-dimensional kernel/cokernel, also not established; higher-order tangent analysis requires actual second differentiability and a solvable infinite-dimensional correction, not merely a nonzero quadratic coefficient. The fixed-gap and flat-control injectivity of the odd temperature derivative does not rule out a nonlinear branch with zero first odd derivative. There is no nonlinear preparation theorem or nonlinear impossibility theorem here.
