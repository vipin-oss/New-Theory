# Energy, entropy and physical admissibility

With \(\epsilon=u_x\), \(w=\epsilon-z\), define \(\eta=c\log(\theta/\theta_*)+b\epsilon\). For a positive classical solution, division of the heat equation by \(\theta\), substitution \(z_s=\lambda w\), and the identity \(\theta_{xx}/\theta=(\theta_x/\theta)_x+\theta_x^2/\theta^2\) give
\[\eta_s-\partial_x(\kappa\theta_x/\theta)=\kappa\theta_x^2/\theta^2+k\lambda w^2/\theta\ge0.\]
One consistent internal energy density, up to a constant and reference conventions, is
\[e=c(\theta-\theta_*)+\tfrac12a\epsilon^2+\tfrac12kw^2+b\theta_*\epsilon.\]
Differentiation, the heat equation and constitutive law give \(e_s=\kappa\theta_{xx}+\sigma\epsilon_s\). Integrating and using \(\sigma_x=0\), \(u(0)=0\), \(u(L)=g_p\),
\[\frac{d}{ds}\int_I e\,dx=\kappa[\theta_x(L)-\theta_x(0)]+\sigma g_p'.\]
Thus reservoirs supply or remove heat and the endpoint performs mechanical work; entropy production is nonnegative but the total entropy of the bar need not increase in isolation. These identities require sufficient traces and positive temperature. They do not establish positivity, global solvability, energy bounds for arbitrary controls, or feasibility of exact terminal restoration. A maximum principle for the scalar heat equation cannot be applied blindly to the nonlocal coupled mass and \(w\)-dependent source. Bounded positive boundary controls are necessary, not sufficient, for a positive interior solution.
