# Physical prior and identifiability

Define \(\mathcal R_{\rm phys}\) using the fully nonlinear positive solutions and exact terminal constraints. Only \(0\in\mathcal R_{\rm phys}\) is established; the intersection with \(V_N\) and nontriviality are open. The new theorem yields a different, precise linear statement: for smooth terminal-flat odd controls, \(\mathcal K\cap V_N=\{0\}\) even without temperature nulling, and with thermal nulling the odd memory is zero. This does not compute \(\mathcal R_{\rm phys}\cap V_N\); nonlinear higher-order branches and even/mean controls remain.

Without temperature nulling, linearized projected reachability can be exact while higher modes remain nonzero. Hence the Cycle 8 finite-prior inverse lower bound cannot be invoked on those states merely because their projection is in \(V_N\). A reachable-prior theorem needs a proven \(H^2\)-regular physical family \(\Psi\), exact thermal compatibility, and a lower bound for \(D(\mathcal F\circ\Psi)\) including every tail and the active nonzero-load base. None is proved. Neither an arbitrary finite prior nor a fixed finite truncation proves a physical prior. Approximate memory alone does not show simultaneous approximate restoration or preserve the frozen initial temperature topology. No quantitative physical preparation-to-observation stability is claimed.

The joint-density theorem in 06 shows that for any odd target one can make both linearized temperature error and memory error arbitrarily small with fixed-gap smooth controls. This establishes a mathematically precise approximate linearized statement absent the exact restoration constraint. The necessary blow-up of the control norms for any nonzero target prevents treating this as small-amplitude nonlinear physical preparation or applying the frozen exact-initial-temperature inverse theorem.
