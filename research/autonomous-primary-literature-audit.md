## Explicit overlap assessment

Finite-dimensional Gram invertibility followed by a bounded perturbation estimate is elementary existing mathematics; the reference exponential-polynomial argument is a classical spectral-moment technique. The new finite-prior bound is a model-specific conditional application to a nonzero-load active base, not an automatically new general theorem. The inspected works show close substantive overlap and stronger conclusions in other settings; none of these inspected passages was shown to imply the exact active-base estimate with its constants. This is **not** evidence of publication-level novelty, and uncaptured prior art remains possible. A broader primary-source audit of finite-dimensional PDE–ODE initial-memory identification, and independent checking of constants and inherited estimates, are unfinished.

## Cycle after `b1ec339`: finite-prior and nonlinear transfer

These additional passages were checked in the accessible primary full texts; the search remains targeted and incomplete.

1. **Eller–Rieder, “Tangential cone condition and Lipschitz stability for the full waveform forward operator in the acoustic regime”, [full text](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000137071/125311819), Appendix C, Lemma C.1(a) and proof (C.5).** This is an exact general overlap: finite-dimensional Banach input, continuously differentiable map and injective derivative at an interior point imply two-point local Lipschitz stability; the proof uses continuity of the derivative and a reverse triangle estimate. Their example is seismic wave parameter inversion, not our delayed thermal endpoint memory, but the **nonlinear transfer principle is subsumed**. Our \(\sigma_N/4\) and \(K_2K_N\)-dependent radius are a direct quantitative specialization, not a new general nonlinear inverse theorem.
2. **Alberti–Santacesaria, [arXiv:1906.10028](https://arxiv.org/html/1906.10028), §2.2 Theorem 1 and §2.3 Theorem 2.** They study \(C^1\) Banach-space forward maps and finite-dimensional priors; Theorem 1 additionally assumes injectivity of the forward map and derivative on a compact convex set, and Theorem 2 studies finite measurements given a full-data Lipschitz estimate. These are not a ready-made proof that our \(L_N\) is injective, but demonstrate that finite-prior and finite-output methodology is already established. We do not conflate their global compact-set hypotheses with our local result.
3. **Kian–Sambou–Soccorsi, [arXiv:1612.07942](https://arxiv.org/html/1612.07942), equation (1.2), §1.1 and Theorem 1.2.** They treat the scalar heat equation with homogeneous initial/boundary conditions and a source \(\sigma(t)\beta(x)\), including \(\sigma=e^{-\mu t}\), and partial boundary flux. The paper explicitly explains invisible general sources and derives logarithmic stability under its source prior. The actual eliminated memory system has additional nonautonomous feedback and \(S(t,x)\), so this source theorem does not directly prove the active-system estimate.
4. **[A Moment–Hankel Rank Method for Identifying the Number of Point Sources in the Heat Equation](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.21815), equations (2.1)–(2.11) and §3.** The primary paper uses boundary heat flux, finite exponential moment representations, Vandermonde/Hankel algebra and perturbation bounds for source counting on a disk. This substantiates close methodological overlap, but different unknowns, geometry, and measurement; no assertion that its theorem proves our PDE estimate.
5. **[Identification of a Point Source in the Heat Equation from Sparse Boundary Measurements](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09248), Theorems 1.1–1.2 and §§2–3.** Point-source location and time amplitude are recovered in a ball or planar simply connected domain from selected boundary points; input and geometry differ from the distributed initial ODE state. Illustrates that sparse-boundary uniqueness and mode methods are well-developed.
6. **[A unified Carleman approach to inverse problems for coupled parabolic and hyperbolic-ODE equations](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.26157), §§1.2 and 2, Theorems 2.3 and 2.5.** Kelvin–Voigt coefficient recovery assumes fixed initial state, an interior dynamic measurement and positive-time Cauchy snapshot; the wave model is different. This is strong prior coupled PDE–ODE inverse analysis, not our boundary-only initial-memory theorem.
7. **Hào–Oanh:** the full text remains inaccessible as previously recorded. Finite-prior parabolic–ODE Gram-perturbation exact overlap and chronological novelty remain unverified; no absence claim is made.

**Research-value gate:** The model-specific finite-prior linear proof is useful, and the nonlinear theorem is rigorously inherited from it plus a general prior principle, subject to the stated base assumptions. It uses a standard Gram/perturbation technique, not a new continuum mechanism. On current evidence it is **routine/model-specific**, not a demonstrated strong new result or a sound standalone specialist-journal claim. A substantially stronger infinite-dimensional theorem or genuinely new continuum mechanism and a deeper exact-overlap audit would be needed; no journal recommendation is made.
