# Continuum-theory discovery: preliminary independent audit

**Research hierarchy:** Principal Researcher: Dr. Vipin Gupta; proposed supervisor and independent scientific auditor: ChatGPT; research scholar and literature investigator: Arena. This document is an investigation, not a manuscript or a claim of endorsement by any named person.

**Search cutoff:** 1 November 2026. **Disposition: no genuinely original foundational theory established.** Five distinct research directions are screened below, but all face substantial prior-art risks. The highest-ranked direction is a research problem, not a validated new theory. The strongest explicit model turns out to be a known micromorphic/internal-variable structure; it is included as a feasibility baseline, not as an invention.

## Scope and evidence limitations

## A. Literature map and unresolved issues

1. **Generalized thermoelasticity:** Lord–Shulman [1] adds heat-flux relaxation; Green–Lindsay [2] formulates a different relaxation theory; Green–Naghdi [3] includes nondissipative thermal waves. A recent thermodynamically consistent strain-rate modification [4] illustrates that variants already exist. Finite speed requires a proof for the full coupled system, not a label.
2. **Spatial nonlocality and higher gradients:** Eringen and Edelen [5], Eringen [6], Mindlin [7], Mindlin–Eshel [8] and Lim et al. [9] cover integral, micromorphic, gradient and combined structures. Integral/differential equivalence depends on domain and constitutive boundary conditions [10,11]; it cannot be assumed from an infinite-domain Green function.
3. **Space–time nonlocality:** Agiasofitou–Lazar [12] explicitly uses a dynamical space–time kernel and Klein–Gordon-type operator, with optic modes. Distance-dependent delay is also explicit in [13]. A 2026 magneto-thermoelastic combination [14] is direct counterevidence against novelty of simply adding spatial nonlocality, memory and electromagnetism.
4. **Memory and rational thermodynamics:** Coleman–Noll [15], Coleman [16], Coleman–Gurtin [17] and Gurtin–Pipkin [18] already treat state, history, internal variables and heat-conduction memory. Memory kernels need admissibility conditions, not arbitrary lag times; [19] studies restrictions and stability. Nonlocal heat conduction in deformable media and objectivity are addressed in [20].
5. **Fractional/hereditary models and analysis:** fractional generalizations and thermodynamic restrictions [21], completely monotone generalized Zener relaxation [22] and a rigorous fractional Zener well-posedness result [23] make a fractional derivative or internal-variable realization alone insufficient novelty. Thermodynamic stability, temporal causality, finite domain of dependence, and exponential decay are distinct claims; even a stable memory system need not be exponentially stable [24].
6. **Electromechanics and semiconductors:** Mindlin's coupled crystal-lattice/electromechanical framework [25], entropy-based semiconductor transport closure [26], and a 2026 photo-thermoelastic density-gradient model [27] already occupy this territory. Any proposal must specify charge conservation, electric power, boundary electrical work and a consistent entropy production.
7. **Identification:** inversion of relaxation spectra from finite noisy measurements is ill-posed [28]. Measurements only at one wavelength cannot identify a general joint space–time kernel; multiple scales and held-out predictions are essential.

**Questions worth investigating, not claims of untouched gaps:** (i) characterize passive, objective nonlocal kernels with a strict cone of influence and valid boundary energy transfer; (ii) characterize which reduced kernels admit finite-speed local realizations; (iii) establish measurable distinctions between different internal-state realizations; (iv) give stable, experimentally identifiable closures for coupled electrical, thermal and mechanical transport; (v) rigorously connect microstructure, boundary conditions, dissipation and wavefronts. Each is a program with prior work, not evidence of a vacant field.

## B. Five concept screens

All equations below are tentative sketches; their value lies in explicit falsifiability and prior-art testing, not branding. Set-ups are independent research questions, not five established theories.

### 1. Finite-speed realization of observable hereditary response (rank 1)

- **Motivation:** determine whether a measured coupled space–time response can be represented by a passive local mediator with a provable front speed, rather than fitting independent spatial and temporal kernels.
- **State and structure:** displacement and a physically interpreted mediator carrying its own inertia and stored gradient energy. A concrete 1-D example is in section E: stress depends on strain and mediator; the mediator obeys a damped wave equation driven by strain.
- **Balances/thermodynamics:** momentum balance and mediator balance; positive mechanical storage gives an energy flux and nonnegative isothermal dissipation. The non-isothermal energy and entropy balances remain to be constructed.
- **Limits/predictions:** decoupling yields classical elasticity; zero mediator gradient gives local resonant internal-variable viscoelasticity. Two coupled branches and attenuation vary jointly with frequency and wavenumber. Neither feature itself is new.
- **Experiment:** use wave transmission at several wavelengths and transient front measurements; fit parameters on one subset, predict the rest, and compare with micromorphic and general positive-real hereditary baselines.
- **Mathematical difficulty:** strict domain of dependence for heterogeneous bounded domains and realizability/identifiability of a general measured kernel.
- **Closest conflict:** [7,12,17,29] already cover key ingredients; [29] gives a dual-variable microinertial equation essentially of this form. The baseline is therefore **not a new theory**. Possible research contribution would be a demonstrably new realization/identification theorem, subject to full audit.

### 2. Conserved interaction-channel energy with retarded transfer (rank 2)

- **Motivation:** a delayed interaction inserted directly into force balance may not reveal where energy resides during transit; investigate a measurable distributed interaction channel.
- **State:** displacement plus a channel field indexed by pair endpoints and a transit coordinate; its energy is part of the system state, not an arbitrary time delay.
- **Structure/equations:** investigate a channel transport equation `w_t + c_b w_a = 0` on `0<a<L(x,y)`, with endpoint coupling chosen from a positive energy functional; momentum balance receives endpoint tractions. This is a design specification, **not a closed constitutive model**: no admissible boundary coupling has yet been derived.
- **Thermodynamics:** require endpoint power cancellation and a positive channel energy; for damping add a nonnegative quadratic dissipation. No full proof is available.
- **Limits/predictions:** instantaneous-interaction and decoupled limits are conjectural and depend on normalization; a distance-dependent transfer delay and transient energy storage are testable, but neither is claimed novel.
- **Validation/difficulty:** pulse transfer between separated inclusions; compare against a resolved waveguide. Pair-counting, angular momentum, objectivity, a continuum of channel states and uniqueness are unresolved.
- **Prior art:** distance-proportional delays [13], space–time kernels [12], and nonlocal thermodynamics [30]. Without a precise energy-conserving construction and comparison to transmission-line/field elimination literature, this direction is unverified.

### 3. Boundary-aware nonlocal storage and exchange (rank 3)

- **Motivation:** bulk convolution models can be misleading at interfaces; seek a single variational bookkeeping of bulk–boundary interaction and experimentally accessible surface energy flow.
- **State/equations:** displacement `u` and a boundary state `z_s`; investigate a total energy `E_bulk[u]+E_surface[z_s]+E_exchange[u,z_s]`. Bulk momentum and boundary evolution would follow from variational derivatives plus a positive boundary mobility. Exact kernels, surface kinematics and admissible endpoint powers remain unspecified.
- **Thermodynamics:** a positive exchange energy and positive mobility are intended to give a global energy inequality, but local entropy and interfacial heat flux are not yet derived.
- **Limits/predictions:** zero exchange decouples surface and bulk; local limits depend on the kernel scaling. Hypothesis: boundary-sensitive relaxation changes with specimen thickness beyond a bulk fit.
- **Test/difficulty:** compare free and coated specimens at matched bulk properties; identify surface versus bulk contributions, higher-order tractions and boundary regularity.
- **Conflict:** non-standard constitutive boundary conditions [10], wave reflection at boundaries [11], second-gradient entropy treatments [31]. Surface energy and nonlocal variational mechanics are established; novelty would require a precise theorem or new experimentally established structure.

### 4. Observable-equivalence theory for memory realizations

- **Motivation:** different internal variables can yield identical measured input–output responses; parameter count and state labels alone are not physical discoveries.
- **State/equations:** a linear state-space realization `z_t=Az+Bu`, `y=Cz+Du`; for a continuum, inputs/outputs may be boundary tractions, velocities and heat fluxes. Define equivalence through equality of accessible transfer operators, not the internal coordinates.
- **Thermodynamics:** require a storage operator `P>0` and a passivity inequality for each realization; balance laws and port choices must be specified for the system being studied. This is an inverse/representation program, not yet a new constitutive field theory.
- **Limits/predictions:** finite-dimensional realizations specialize to standard internal-variable models; equivalence predicts that unobserved coordinates cannot be uniquely inferred without independent measurements. General realization theory already makes this unsurprising.
- **Experiment/difficulty:** compare multiple boundary excitations and measured wave numbers; prove identifiability or quantify a nullspace in an admissible model class. Infinite-dimensional minimality, noise and inaccessible modes are central obstacles.
- **Conflict:** internal-variable thermodynamics [17,21], hereditary representations [16,18], and ill-posed spectrum inversion [28]. A convincing contribution needs a new continuum-specific equivalence/measurement theorem; this is not a standalone foundational material theory as stated.

### 5. Charge–heat–mechanical dissipation allocation under partial observation

- **Motivation:** the same observed electrical attenuation may arise from carrier relaxation, thermal exchange or mechanical work; investigate whether separate dissipations can be identified under coupled forcing.
- **State/equations:** mechanical displacement, carrier density `n`, lattice energy and electrical potential; `n_t+div J=0`, Poisson's equation, momentum balance and total-energy balance. Set thermodynamic forces from a free energy and coupled fluxes through a positive semidefinite Onsager matrix; reaction terms and full electrical work must be specified for a closed model.
- **Thermodynamics:** the Onsager quadratic form is nonnegative if the force–flux signs and field energy are treated consistently; this is a design constraint, not a derived full entropy inequality.
- **Limits/predictions:** zero cross-couplings gives uncoupled transport and elasticity; quasistatic carriers may yield drift–diffusion under additional assumptions. A frequency-dependent partition of dissipation is a hypothesis, not a distinctive prediction yet.
- **Experiment/difficulty:** time-resolved optical/electrical/strain measurements, with independent carrier and temperature calibrations. Objective electric stress, electromagnetic boundary work, carrier positivity, interfaces and non-identifiability remain unresolved.
- **Conflict:** [25–27] and general electroelastic rational thermodynamics [32]. The state variables and Onsager coupling are established; reject as a new theory absent a sharply different principle.

## D. Ranking

Conditional qualitative ordering: **1 > 2 > 3**. Direction 1 ranks highest in mathematical feasibility because section E has an explicit positive energy and dispersion relation, but its originality is low as a constitutive model; its potential significance depends on a new theorem. Direction 2 has potential conceptual significance but no closed model, proof or credible novelty determination. Direction 3 has a concrete boundary problem but extensive prior work. These ranks are triage priorities, not validated scores or claims comparable to foundational historical theories.

## E. Strongest candidate: explicit baseline and feasibility

Consider a one-dimensional homogeneous small-strain isothermal body, no body force. Let `u(x,t)` be displacement, `e=u_x`, and `z(x,t)` a dimensionless microdeformation. Parameters satisfy `rho,m,C,b,d>0`, `gamma>=0`. Define mechanical energy per reference length

```
W = 1/2 C e² + 1/2 b(e-z)² + 1/2 d z_x²,
K = 1/2 rho u_t² + 1/2 m z_t²,
sigma = C e + b(e-z),
rho u_tt = sigma_x,
m z_tt + gamma z_t = b(e-z) + d z_xx.
```

Here `m` is a microinertial coefficient and `d` a gradient stiffness; their units follow from the energy per length. These are 1-D reference-coordinate equations, not an objective three-dimensional finite-deformation constitutive law. In 3-D, tensor character, material symmetry and objective kinematics must be specified separately.

**Derived energy identity:** for smooth solutions,

```
∂t(K+W) = ∂x(sigma u_t + d z_x z_t) - gamma z_t².
```

Thus periodic boundaries, or boundaries with zero total mechanical power `sigma u_t+d z_x z_t`, give nonincreasing integrated mechanical energy. This proves a formal isothermal dissipation inequality for this model, not full thermodynamic consistency: a heat bath or heat equation must receive `gamma z_t²` and supply entropy production `gamma z_t²/T` at positive temperature, with consistent heat and boundary fluxes. W is strictly positive in `(e,z,z_x)` under the stated conditions; rigid translations remain a zero-energy mode. This is not an isolated total-energy conservation law unless the bath is included.

For modes proportional to `exp(st+ikx)`, the determinant is

```
[rho s² + (C+b)k²] [m s² + gamma s + b + d k²] - b² k² = 0.
```

The formal energy identity excludes growing eigenmodes with nonzero positive energy for real `k`; it does not prove uniform decay, nonlinear stability or full well-posedness. For smooth data the principal wave speeds are `sqrt((C+b)/rho)` and `sqrt(d/m)`; finite propagation is expected from the local hyperbolic principal part and can be proved by a suitable local energy estimate, but that proof is not given here. A causal initial-value problem is not by itself a demonstrated finite-front theorem. A fully specified domain, initial data and boundary conditions are necessary for a semigroup existence and uniqueness proof.

Eliminating `z` for zero initial mediator data gives the effective modulus

```
M(s,k) = C+b - b²/(m s²+gamma s+b+d k²),
sigma_hat = M(s,k) e_hat.
```

The denominator couples space and time through a common mediator; general initial conditions add forcing terms, so the expression alone is not the complete initial-value solution. This rational form is a **special** dynamically nonlocal kernel, not a general characterization or novelty claim. `b=0` decouples classical elasticity; `d=0` yields pointwise resonant microstructure, not automatically Lord–Shulman or Green–Naghdi. `m=0` is a singular limit, generally parabolic when `gamma,d>0`, so finite speed does not automatically survive. No classical thermoelastic limit is claimed for this isothermal toy model.

**Term-by-term prior-art check available now:** [29] explicitly displays `I alpha_tt = C alpha_xx - A u_x - B alpha`; the mediator gradient, microinertia, self-restoring term and strain forcing are already present, up to coefficient/sign conventions. [7] supplies micromorphic elasticity; [12] uses a dynamical nonlocal operator, including internal space and time scales and optical modes. [17] supplies internal-state thermodynamics. These comparisons disqualify the displayed model as a foundational discovery. Whether any proposed *realizability theorem* is new requires checking full texts and control/realization literature.

## F. Critical audit and verification plan

- The seemingly attractive claim “new theory of finite-speed spatiotemporal nonlocality” is contradicted by [12,13]; adding magnetism or a heat memory kernel also has direct antecedent [14].
- The toy mediator's structure is substantially anticipated by [29]. The source establishes structural overlap, not a complete equivalence between all possible coefficients, boundary conditions or experiments.
- Claimed novelty must be compared term by term, including internal-state elimination, coordinate transformations, limiting processes, boundary conditions, initial histories and observable transfer functions. Analogous mathematical forms under different labels must count as prior art.
- For each surviving concept, compile 10–20 closest primary works using Scholar, Crossref, Scopus/Web of Science and cited-by searches where accessible. Archive query strings, dates, complete texts or access restrictions, equation numbers and exact excerpts. The present bibliography is a **shared initial map**, not 10–20 audited primary papers for each candidate.
- Search additional terms before any claim: `passive realization spatially dispersive continuum finite propagation`, `micromorphic dual internal variables hyperbolic dissipation`, `retarded nonlocal interaction energy transmission line`, `nonlocal constitutive boundary thermodynamics`, and `identifiability distributed parameter passive realization`. Screen patents and adjacent electrical/network theory, too.

## G. Roadmap and stop criteria

1. Complete the documented primary-source audit first. **Stop** if the proposed theorem or constitutive structure is already stated or follows routinely from known results.
2. State one precise question: for example, under explicitly stated regularity, symmetry and measurements, which passive spatiotemporal transfer operators admit a finite-speed local realization? Establish whether existence, minimality or an obstruction is genuinely open; arbitrary passive kernels need not have such a realization.
3. Define admissible domains, boundary ports, state space and notion of causality. Derive energy and entropy balances, including boundary and initial-history terms, before introducing constitutive parameters.
4. Prove existence, uniqueness and continuous dependence in a specified energy space; derive a local energy/domain-of-dependence estimate; distinguish Lyapunov stability, decay rate and front speed. Identify counterexamples and singular limits.
5. Produce at least one result beyond routine recasting: a nontrivial necessity/sufficiency theorem, impossibility result, or experimentally discriminating invariant, with precise comparison against prior realizations. Derive full dispersion and quantify parameter identifiability.
6. Pre-register an experiment spanning frequency and wavelength. Fit established baselines with comparable flexibility; evaluate held-out transient front, attenuation and boundary-response data. Test noise sensitivity and profile likelihoods; do not infer uniqueness from fit quality.
7. Only after successful literature, mathematical and experimental gates consider a foundational journal paper. If any gate fails, publish a narrower mathematical result or negative audit rather than label a combination a new theory.
