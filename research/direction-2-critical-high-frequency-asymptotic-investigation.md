## 3. Exact mode and neighboring-mode residual

Let \(P_n^a=e^{-\mu t}\phi_n\), \(A_n=e^{-\mu t}S\phi_n/(\kappa q_n^2)\). \(A_n\) is in the strong class, has zero **spatial** endpoint values, but \(A_n(0)=S(0)\phi_n/(\kappa q_n^2)\) need not vanish. Define the first-equation residual with the PDE's left-minus-right convention:
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_n^a&=M(A_n)_t-\kappa(A_n)_{xx}-BA_n-SP_n^a\\
&=\frac{e^{-\mu t}}{\kappa q_n^2}
\{[M(S_t-\mu S)-BS-\kappa S_{xx}]\phi_n-2\kappa S_x\phi_n'\}. \tag{6}
\end{aligned}
\]
Indeed \((S\phi_n)_{xx}=S_{xx}\phi_n+2S_x\phi_n'-q_n^2S\phi_n\), so the last term exactly cancels \(SP_n^a\). The ODE residual of \((A_n,P_n^a)\) is \(-\gamma A_n\). Thus for \(e_n=Y_n-A_n\), \(p_n=P_n-P_n^a\), the error sources are \((-F_n^a,\gamma A_n)\) and initial data \((-A_n(0),0)\).

Write \(Q_n=q_{n+1}/q_n=(n+1)/n\), \(D_S=M(S_t-\mu S)-BS-\kappa S_{xx}\). The exact neighboring trial \(A_n-Q_nA_{n+1}\), \(P_n^a-Q_nP_{n+1}^a=e^{-\mu t}z_n\), has
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_z^a={c_Le^{-\mu t}\over\kappa}\{&D_S[\sin(q_nx)/q_n^2-Q_n\sin(q_{n+1}x)/q_{n+1}^2]\\
&-2\kappa S_x[\cos(q_nx)/q_n-Q_n\cos(q_{n+1}x)/q_{n+1}]\}. \tag{7}
\end{aligned}
\]
Since \(Q_n/q_{n+1}=1/q_n\), its leading oscillatory bracket is **exactly**
\[
-\frac{2c_Le^{-\mu t}}{q_n}S_x[\cos(q_nx)-\cos(q_{n+1}x)]. \tag{8}
\]
The difference is not uniformly small in the interior: \(q_{n+1}-q_n=2\pi/L\), a fixed wavenumber. It vanishes at both endpoints but its amplitude can remain order one away from them. Thus simply replacing \(Q_n\) by one or claiming the two cosines converge together is invalid. The trace of the first trial cancels exactly:
\(2A_0\kappa\partial_x(A_n-Q_nA_{n+1})(0)=a_J/q_n-Q_na_J/q_{n+1}=0\), where \(a_J=2A_0c_Le^{-\mu t}S(t,0)\).

For \(a\in H^1(I)\), testing against \(v\in H^1_0(I)\) and integrating by parts gives
\[
\|a\cos(q_nx)\|_{H^{-1}}\le Cq_n^{-1}\|a\|_{H^1}. \tag{9}
\]
Therefore (6) has \(L^2\)-norm \(O(q_n^{-1})\) and \(L^2_tH^{-1}_x\)-norm \(O(q_n^{-2})\), with \(S_t,S_{xx}\in L^2\), \(S\in C_tH^1_x\subset L^\infty\), and \(B\in L^2\). The original two-level trace argument uses these exact estimates; no \(S_{xx}(t,0)\) trace exists under (4).

## 4. Main theorem: source approximation plus delayed cutoff

### 4.1 Auxiliary source without changing the actual coefficients

Fix the actual \(M,B,S,\gamma,\mu\). For an even amplitude \(f\in\mathcal A\), define the **auxiliary linear forcing problem**
\[
MY_t-\kappa Y_{xx}=BY+Sp+e^{-\mu t}f\phi_n,\qquad
p_t+\mu p=\gamma Y,\qquad Y(0)=p(0)=0,\quad Y|_{\partial I}=0. \tag{10}
\]
The feedback coefficient is always the actual \(S\); \(f\) is merely a source in a superposition argument. For \(f=S\), (10) is exactly the actual problem after setting \(p=P-e^{-\mu t}\phi_n\), including all pre-delay memory. Other \(f\)'s are not physical active bases or experimental substitutions. The strong estimate gives a unique odd solution for each source.

Set \(A_n(f)=e^{-\mu t}f\phi_n/(\kappa q_n^2)\) and define
\[
\rho_n(f)=2A_0\kappa\partial_x(Y_n(f)-A_n(f))(0,\cdot)|_J. \tag{11}
\]
The earlier weak-energy calculation, independently checked in the prior audit, states for strong odd solutions with sources \(F,G\in L^2\)
\[
\|y\|_{L^\infty L^2}+\|y\|_{L^2V}+\|p\|_{L^\infty V'}
\le C(\|y_0\|_2+\|p_0\|_{V'}+\|F\|_{L^2V'}+\|G\|_{L^2V'}). \tag{12}
\]
The proof tests the divided parabolic equation against \(y\), the ODE against the Dirichlet inverse of \(p\), and uses \(\|B(t)\|_2^{4/3}\in L^1_t\); it does not create a new solution theory for \(V'\) data. The product \(1/M\) is a bounded \(H^1\) multiplier on \(V\) and its dual.

The residual of \(A_n(f)\) is (6) with **only** the source amplitude \(S\) replaced by \(f\); the feedback \(S\) remains fixed in the error equations. Using (9), (12), (5), and \(\mathcal A\hookrightarrow C_tH^1_x\), one obtains, uniformly in \(n\),
\[
\|Y_n(f)-A_n(f)\|_{L^2H^1}\le Cq_n^{-2}\|f\|_{\mathcal A},\quad
\|Y_n(f)-A_n(f)\|_{L^2H^2}\le Cq_n^{-1}\|f\|_{\mathcal A}. \tag{13}
\]
Here \(\|f\|_{\mathcal A}=\|f_t\|_{L^2L^2}+\|f\|_{L^2H^2}+\|f\|_{L^2L^2}\); the continuous embedding controls \(\sup_t\|f(t)\|_{H^1}\), the initial mismatch, and \(\|Bf\|_2\). The \(G=\gamma A_n(f)\) term and zero initial error-memory are retained. The multiplicative trace inequality therefore gives the **uniform approximation bound**
\[
\|\rho_n(f)-\rho_n(g)\|_Z\le Cq_n^{-3/2}\|f-g\|_{\mathcal A}. \tag{14}
\]
Linearity in the source is essential. This estimate is valid for the actual fixed coefficients, not for an uncontrolled change of the PDE's feedback coefficient.

### 4.2 Smooth-source corrector and exact cutoff estimate

Now take \(f\in C^\infty([0,T]\times[0,L])\), even in \(x\). Let \(\ell(x)=1-2x/L\). Define
\[
C_n(f)={2c_Le^{-\mu t}\over\kappa q_n^3}\left[f_x(t,x)\cos(q_nx)-f_x(t,0)\ell(x)\right]. \tag{15}
\]
Since \(f_x(t,L)=-f_x(t,0)\) and \(\cos(q_nL)=1\), \(C_n(f)\) is odd and has zero spatial endpoint values. Its leading spatial contribution is
\(-\kappa(C_n)_{xx}=+2c_Le^{-\mu t}f_x\cos(q_nx)/q_n+O_f(q_n^{-2})\) in \(L^2_{t,x}\), exactly canceling (6)'s leading cosine. The remaining terms include \(f_{xxx}\cos/q_n^3\), \(f_{xx}\sin/q_n^2\), \(M(C_n)_t\), \(BC_n\), and the linear \(\ell\) correction. They are in \(L^2\) with norm \(O_f(q_n^{-2})\), because **f is smooth**, \(M\in L^\infty\), \(B\in L^2\). Neither \(M_t\) nor \(B_x\) is differentiated. Also
\[
\|A_n(f)+C_n(f)\|_{L^2_{t,x}}\le C_fq_n^{-2},\quad
\|(A_n+C_n)(0)\|_2\le C_fq_n^{-2},\quad
\|(A_n+C_n)(0)\|_{H^1}\le C_fq_n^{-1}. \tag{16}
\]
Let \(e=Y_n(f)-A_n(f)-C_n(f)\), with error memory \(p\) from (10). The source pair is \((F,G)=(-\operatorname{Res}(A_n+C_n),\gamma(A_n+C_n))\) and initial data \((-(A_n+C_n)(0),0)\). Thus \(\|F\|_2+\|G\|_2\le C_fq_n^{-2}\). Applying (12) gives
\[
\|e\|_{L^\infty L^2}+\|e\|_{L^2H^1}\le C_fq_n^{-2}. \tag{17}
\]
The ODE is \(p_t+\mu p=\gamma(e+A_n+C_n)\), \(p(0)=0\); its explicit convolution and (16)–(17) give
\[
\|p\|_{L^2_{t,x}}\le C_fq_n^{-2}. \tag{18}
\]
This uses the *whole* interval \((0,T)\), not an artificially reset state at \(\delta\).

Choose smooth \(\chi\) with \(\chi=0\) near \(t=0\), \(\chi=1\) on \([\delta,T]\), and \(\operatorname{supp}\chi'\subset(0,\delta)\). The pair \((\chi e,\chi p)\) has zero initial data, and its sources in the **same actual operator** are
\[
\chi F+M\chi'e,\qquad \chi G+\chi'p. \tag{19}
\]
Equations (16)–(18) and boundedness of \(M,\chi'\) bound both sources in \(L^2\) by \(C_{f,\delta}q_n^{-2}\). Applying the inherited strong estimate (5) yields
\[
\|e\|_{L^2(\delta,T;H^2)}\le C_{f,\delta}q_n^{-2}. \tag{20}
\]
The ordinary \(H^2\) derivative trace therefore has this rate. Since smooth \(f\) satisfies
\[
\partial_xC_n(f)(t,0)={2c_Le^{-\mu t}\over\kappa q_n^3}
[f_{xx}(t,0)+2f_x(t,0)/L], \tag{21}
\]
its trace is \(O_f(q_n^{-3})\), and
\[
\boxed{\|\rho_n(f)\|_Z\le C_{f,\delta}q_n^{-2}\quad\text{for each smooth even }f.} \tag{22}
\]
The constant can diverge as \(\delta\downarrow0\) or as the approximating \(f\)'s derivatives grow. Equation (21) is used **only for smooth auxiliary f**, never as an alleged trace of the actual \(S_{xx}\).

### 4.3 Density and the critical limit

Smooth even functions on the closed cylinder are dense in \(\mathcal A\). One construction: apply a fixed extension operator in \(x\), bounded on both \(L^2\) and \(H^2\); reflect in time across the endpoints, mollify in space and time, then restrict and average with spatial reflection. This converges in \(H^1_tL^2_x\cap L^2_tH^2_x\). The standard Hilbert interpolation/trace embedding \(\mathcal A\hookrightarrow C_tH^1_x\) also gives convergence of initial \(H^1\) values and of the \(L^\infty_{t,x}\) multiplier needed for \(Bf\). This approximation does **not** impose Neumann or Dirichlet endpoint conditions on \(S\).

Fix \(\varepsilon>0\), choose smooth even \(f\) with \(\|S-f\|_{\mathcal A}<\varepsilon\), and combine (14) and (22):
\[
q_n^{3/2}\|\rho_n(S)\|_Z\le C\varepsilon+C_{f,\delta}q_n^{-1/2}.
\]
First let \(n\to\infty\), then \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\). This proves the first claim of (1). Finally \(\mathcal Tz_n=\rho_n(S)-Q_n\rho_{n+1}(S)\), with \(Q_n\to1\) and \(q_{n+1}/q_n\to1\), proving the second claim. □

**Why this is not circular.** We never assert \(q_n^2e_n\) is compact, never exchange \(n\) and the approximation index, never estimate an unavailable endpoint trace of \(S_{xx}\), and never throw away the initial or pre-delay memory. The approximation constant in (14) is uniform in \(n\); the smooth corrector constant in (22) is allowed to depend on the fixed approximant. The inherited forward estimate remains an explicit unverified hypothesis, not something proved by this argument.

## 5. Consequences, rate limits and other approaches

### 5.1 Linearized endpoint and stronger weak singular decay

\(\|z_n\|_2\asymp1\), \(\|z_n\|_{H^2}\asymp q_n^2\). Put \(h_n=c_Rq_n^{-2}z_n\) in any fixed nonzero \(H^2\) ball. From (1), \(\|\mathcal Th_n\|=o(q_n^{-7/2})\) while \(\|h_n\|_2\asymp q_n^{-2}\). If \(\|h\|_2\le C\|\mathcal Th\|^{4/7}\) held there, its right-hand side would be \(o(q_n^{-2})\), a contradiction. Thus **all \(\alpha\ge4/7\)** are excluded for the full linearized \(L^2\)-state on the ball. For any \(s>0\), the same argument excludes the endpoint \(s/(s+3/2)\) and every larger exponent on an \(H^s\) ball. This does not establish the optimal remaining exponent or a modulo-kernel lower bound.

For singular values, (1) gives \(\epsilon_N:=\sup_{n\ge N}n^{3/2}\|\rho_n\|\to0\). Repeating the dyadic proof audited in §2 on the tail replaces every block estimate by \(\epsilon_N\) times its earlier bound. The first \(N\) columns and rank-one leading term have rank \(N+1\); the dyadic approximant has total rank \(O(N)\) and error \(C\epsilon_NN^{-3/2}\). Consequently
\[
s_n(\mathcal T)=o(n^{-3/2}). \tag{23}
\]
This is the zero subspace of weak \(\mathcal S_{2/3,\infty}\), stronger than mere weak membership. It still does not establish \(\mathcal S_{2/3}\): abstract diagonal \(n^{-3/2}/\log(n+1)\) has little-o singular decay but \(\sum s_n^{2/3}=\infty\). It does not show an actual sharp lower bound.

### 5.2 Three endpoint methods

1. **Neighboring-mode construction:** (1) directly gives the endpoint contradiction above. It does not require any fixed-mode Gram invertibility.
2. **Singular subspaces:** (23) gives another endpoint proof. In \(E_N=\operatorname{span}\{\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_{2N}\}\), choose a unit \(L^2\) vector orthogonal to the first \(N\) right singular vectors. Its \(H^2\)-norm is \(O(N^2)\), and its output is at most \(s_{N+1}(\mathcal T)=o(N^{-3/2})\). Scaling by \(N^{-2}\) contradicts exponent \(4/7\). This is independent of adjacent-mode algebra **after** (23), but relies on the same PDE estimate.
3. **Actual adjoint controls:** the established transposition system has \(\mathcal T^*f=\Pi_o w_f(0)\) with full pre-delay history. If a boundary-control family supplied a uniform lower estimate of order \(q_n^{-3/2}\) on every normalized high-frequency state, it would contradict (1). For fixed \(n\), the exact finite-control leakage identity from the prior report remains valid, but no controls have been proved to have vanishing leakage as \(N\to\infty\). Endpoint instability does not settle adjoint density or actual injectivity. No false lower-bound/control construction is claimed.

### 5.3 What the proof does not give

The smooth auxiliary corrector proves \(O(q_n^{-2})\) for each fixed smooth amplitude, but the constants need not be uniform as it approaches actual \(S\). There is no general rate from density alone. A *quantitative* sufficient condition is an approximation family \(f_m\) with \(\|S-f_m\|_{\mathcal A}\le Cm^{-a}\) and \(C_{f_m,\delta}\le Cm^b\): balancing (14) and (22) gives an explicit improvement beyond \(q^{-3/2}\). The inherited assumptions supply no such powers, and an extra spatial \(H^{2+\epsilon}\) assumption alone does not automatically control the temporal approximation of \(S_t\). A classical boundary symbol, \(S_{xx}(t,0)\), and an actual next coefficient remain unproved. A stretched coordinate \(\xi=q_nx\) suggests a boundary oscillation on scale \(q_n^{-1}\), but the proof uses the exact corrector (15), not a justified full boundary-layer expansion. The cutoff constant may diverge as \(\delta\downarrow0\); the claimed little-o is for each fixed \(\delta>0\), not uniformly down to the initial time.

Initial-mismatch-only, distributed-residual-only and memory-only problems can be separated by linearity; (17)–(20) control their delayed sum. They do not yield a general ordering or a nonzero leading term. Weak convergence of normalized errors alone does not prove trace little-o; compact embeddings in the interior do not by themselves control derivative traces. An actual spectral lower bound, an exact leading coefficient or a sharp stability modulus remains a separate problem.

## 6. Actual \(S_x\): what follows and what does not

The actual base equations yield the exact weak identity and Volterra history
\[
S_x=\lambda\alpha[-br(y_b)_x+2k\alpha(p_b)_x],\qquad
(p_b)_x(t)=\gamma\int_0^te^{-\mu(t-r)}(y_b)_x(r)dr. \tag{24}
\]
Under the inherited strong base class, \(y_{b,x}\in L^2_tH^1_x\), so its boundary trace is in \(L^2_t\), and the Bochner integral commutes with this bounded trace. In particular, writing \(u(t)=(y_b)_x(t,0)\),
\[
S_x(t,0)=\lambda\alpha[-br\,u(t)+2k\alpha\gamma\int_0^te^{-\mu(t-r)}u(r)dr] \quad\text{a.e.} \tag{25}
\]
This is the exact boundary integral equation, with the full early history. Reflection gives \(S_x(t,L)=-S_x(t,0)\), not zero. The initial base gives \(y_b\to0\) in \(H^1\), so \(S_x(t)\to0\) in spatial \(L^2\); an initial value or Taylor expansion of the *boundary trace* does not follow, because endpoint evaluation of an \(L^2_x\) derivative is not continuous. The analyticity of the prescribed load does not by itself establish time analyticity of \(u\) or \(S_x(t,0)\).

Even if \(S_x(t,0)=0\) for a.e. \(t\in J\), (25) is an integral equation with an unknown pre-delay contribution. No theorem here rules this out. Nonvanishing somewhere in the full cylinder does not imply a lower bound on \(J\), nor a sign. The active base heat equation gives \(y_b=0\) at the boundary, not \((y_b)_x\) or its sign; a coupled strong maximum principle and an experimentally verified small-load bound would need separate proof. No positive \(c_*\) or symbolic nondegeneracy regime has been established. Importantly, (1) does not require any such condition.

## 7. Adjoint and exact identifiability

For boundary control \(f\in L^2(J)\), the transposition equations are
\(-\partial_t(Mz)-\kappa z_{xx}-Bz-\gamma w=0\), \(-w_t+\mu w-Sz=0\), \(w(T)=0\), with boundary input encoded by the verified weak forcing functional; then \(\mathcal T^*f=\Pi_o w(0)\). The adjoint propagates into \((0,\delta)\); no delayed snapshot is independently known. The finite projection control for a fixed column has exact leakage \(\sum_{j>N}|(\rho_j,f_{n,N})|^2\). The new little-o is a columnwise upper estimate, not a bound on these control-weighted sums, whose norms can grow rapidly. Hence it neither proves density of \(\operatorname{ran}\mathcal T^*\) nor constructs a nonzero kernel. Output completeness, linearized injectivity and nonlinear injectivity remain unknown.

## 8. Nonlinear endpoint: carefully conditional

The actual nonlinear map \(\mathcal F\) has not been proved differentiable on an open admissible \(H^2\) neighborhood with a uniformly quadratic remainder. Its exact equations yield products such as \(-d\,uQ u_t-bu\Delta E+k\lambda(\Delta w)^2\); controlling them requires an actual nonlinear solution difference estimate, inversion of the full nonlocal mass operator, positive temperature and a full-space forced trace bound. No such claim follows just from (5).

Assume additionally that all sufficiently small odd \(H^2\) perturbations used below are admissible and that
\(\mathcal F(h)-\mathcal F(0)=\mathcal Th+\mathcal N(h)\),
\(\|\mathcal N(h)\|_Z\le C\|h\|_{H^2}^2\). Put \(\lambda_n=q_n^{3/2}\|\mathcal Tz_n\|\to0\) and \(a_n=\max\{\lambda_n,q_n^{-1}\}\to0\). Take \(h_n=a_nq_n^{-11/2}z_n\). Then \(\|h_n\|_2\asymp a_nq_n^{-11/2}\), \(\|h_n\|_{H^2}=O(a_nq_n^{-7/2})\to0\), and
\[
\|\mathcal F(h_n)-\mathcal F(0)\|\le C a_n(\lambda_n+a_n)q_n^{-7}. \tag{26}
\]
At exponent \(11/14\), the powers of \(q_n\) cancel, while the required recovery ratio is bounded below by a constant times \(a_n^{1-2(11/14)}\to\infty\). Thus the *conditional* quadratic-remainder theorem now excludes the endpoint \(11/14\) as well as larger exponents. It does **not** establish nonlinear failure at \(4/7\).

Under the stronger, separately unverified tame bound \(\|\mathcal N(h)\|\le C(\|h\|_2^2+\|h\|_4^2)\), choose \(h_n=\varepsilon q_n^{-2}z_n\) with small fixed \(\varepsilon\). Then \(\|\mathcal N(h_n)\|=O(\varepsilon^2q_n^{-4})=o(q_n^{-7/2})\), while the linear output is also \(o(q_n^{-7/2})\). Hence a nonlinear \(4/7\) estimate fails **under that additional bound and admissibility**. Fixed \(\varepsilon\) makes the perturbations small in \(H^2\), not convergent there; if convergence is required, a slowly decreasing amplitude can be chosen using the actual little-o sequence. For the full force-plus-two-flux observation, each component's derivative and nonlinear remainder must be independently bounded; the odd flux-difference theorem cannot alone exclude stability using extra channels. Nonlinear injectivity can coexist with compact derivative and instability.
