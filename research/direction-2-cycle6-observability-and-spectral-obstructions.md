# Cycle 6: negative-norm observability versus compactness

**Result: conditionally impossible for \(0<s\le3/2\) in the active graph regime; impossible for every finite \(s\) in the constant reference.** Actual qualitative injectivity remains OPEN.

Use \(H=H_o\), \(Z=L^2(J)\), the exact \(O=\mathcal R_\delta(G,I)=\mathcal T D_\delta^{-1}\) and the combined small-load conditions of `direction-2-cycle5-combined-threshold-and-status.md`. The conditional endpoint and Volkov theorem give \(\sum_n n^2s_n(\mathcal T)^2<\infty\); the ideal property yields
\[\sum_{n\ge1}n^2s_n(O)^2<\infty.\tag{1}\]
Let \(E_s:H_o\to H^{-s}(I)\) denote the canonical injection, where \(H^{-s}\) is explicitly the Hilbert dual of \(H^s(I)\). On odd functions its singular values obey \(s_n(E_s)\asymp n^{-s}\). This is the standard one-dimensional Sobolev embedding approximation-number estimate, restricted to the midpoint-odd sector: the upper bound follows from interval extension and Fourier approximation; the lower bound follows by restricting to odd reflected functions supported away from the endpoints and using scaling on disjoint subintervals. There are order \(n\) independent such functions at scale \(1/n\), yielding the matching bound. No identification with a Neumann spectral scale at high orders is assumed, since its boundary conditions can change the Sobolev domain. The same \(n^{-s}\) asymptotic holds in the Dirichlet spectral negative scale, but the two individual norms need not agree for high \(s\); no claim is made that each Dirichlet sine has norm \(n^{-s}\) in the dual of unrestricted \(H^s\).

If \(\|E_sp\|\le C\|Op\|\) for all \(p\), then \(K(Op)=E_sp\) is well-defined and bounded on \(\operatorname{ran}O\), extending to its closure and by zero on the orthogonal complement. Hence \(E_s=KO\) and \(s_n(E_s)\le C s_n(O)\). Using (1),
\[\infty>\sum n^2s_n(O)^2\ge C^{-2}\sum n^2s_n(E_s)^2\asymp\sum n^{2-2s}.\]
The last series converges only for \(s>3/2\). Thus the Cycle 5 target is **ruled out for all \(0<s\le3/2\)** under the inherited conditional endpoint and graph hypotheses; this is a genuine restriction, not a proof of noninjectivity. For \(s>3/2\), an upper singular-value estimate alone supplies no lower bound or singular-vector alignment, so neither existence nor failure of the inequality follows.

For reference \(O_0\), the independent rank approximation in `direction-2-cycle6-reference-infinite-mode-injectivity.md` shows \(s_n(O_0)=O_k(n^{-k})\) for every \(k\). The same factorization would force \(n^{-s}\lesssim n^{-k}\) for arbitrarily large \(k\), impossible for every finite \(s\). Hence no finite-order negative-norm bound holds even though \(O_0\) is injective. This is a decisive reason to prioritize qualitative \(Op=0\Rightarrow p=0\), not polynomial observability.

The leading rank-one term is compatible with (1) and with signed cancellations; an upper bound does not specify singular vectors. Compactness alone does not rule out all negative-order inequalities: the abstract diagonal map \(n^{-s}\) from \(\ell^2\) to itself exactly realizes one. Dense \(\operatorname{ran}O^*\) is equivalent to injection, not exact range or a bounded inverse. No conclusion here constructs an actual invisible state or proves nonlinear failure.
