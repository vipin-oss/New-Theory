# Cycle 7: exact boundary channels, parity, traces and adjoints

The frozen model has \(u(0,t)=0\), \(u(L,t)=g(t)\), \(\theta(0,t)=\theta(L,t)=\theta_*\). Let \(\alpha=a/(a+k)\), \(d=b^2/(a+k)\), \(r=k/(a+k)\), \(\beta=b/(a+k)\), \(\mu=\lambda a/(a+k)\), \(\gamma=\lambda\beta\). Set \(Q=I-\text{mean}\). At the even active base, for a general perturbation \(h\in H^2(I)\), write \(\nu=\delta\bar z=e^{-\lambda t}\bar h\), \(P=Q\delta z\), \(Y=\delta\theta\). The exact linearized equations, with \(M_b^Q=cI+d\theta_bQ\), are
\[M_b^QY_t-\kappa Y_{xx}=-dYQy_{b,t}-b(YE_b+\theta_b rP_t)+2k\lambda w_b(-\nu-\alpha P+\beta QY),\quad P_t=-\mu P+\gamma QY,\tag{1}\]
with \(Y(0)=0\), \(P(0)=Qh\), homogeneous thermal boundary values. Reflection invariance separates \(h=h_e+h_o\). The forward derivative on the \(H^2\) neighborhood and its output traces are bounded by the inherited strong \(C^2\) solution map; the odd heat-flux derivative extends boundedly to \(H_o\).

Choose signed **outward** heat powers \(Q_L=A_0\kappa Y_x(0,t)\), \(Q_R=-A_0\kappa Y_x(L,t)\), and force \(F=A_0\delta\sigma=-A_0[b\bar Y+k\nu]\). The strong temperature class gives \(Y_x(0),Y_x(L)\in L^2(J)\); \(F\in C([0,T])\) and is bounded in \(L^2(J)\). For odd \(h\), \(Y,P\) are odd and \(\nu=\bar Y=0\), so
\[F=0,\quad Q_R=-Q_L,\quad Q_L-Q_R=\mathcal T h=2A_0\kappa Y_x(0),\quad Q_L+Q_R=0.\tag{2}\]
For even \(h\), reflection gives \(Y_x(L)=-Y_x(0)\), hence \(Q_L=Q_R\), and only the sum can see that heat channel; force may also respond. Thus the sum/difference change of coordinates is invertible on the two raw fluxes but does not itself add information. On odd \(H_o\), with \(Z=L^2(J)\), the adjoint of \(h\mapsto(Q_L,Q_R,F)=(\mathcal T h/2,-\mathcal T h/2,0)\) is
\[(f_L,f_R,f_F)\mapsto\mathcal T^*((f_L-f_R)/2),\quad \ker=\ker\mathcal T.\tag{3}\]
The exact \(\mathcal T^*\) is the full-history transposition adjoint in `direction-2-exact-adjoint-and-observability.md`, not an assumed classical boundary adjoint. For the full \(H^2\) domain use the standard reflection-invariant \(H^2\) inner product; the odd adjoint below is the \(H^2\)-adjoint of the restriction of \(\mathcal T\), not its \(L^2\)-adjoint. For the even sector let \(\mathcal H_e h=Q_L+Q_R\), \(\mathcal F_e h=F\); the full observation has kernel \(\ker(\mathcal H_e,\mathcal F_e)\oplus(X_o\cap\ker\mathcal T)\), and adjoint \(\Pi_e(\mathcal H_e^*f_++\mathcal F_e^*f_F)+\Pi_o\mathcal T^*f_-\), with duals defined by the bounded forward maps. No claim that the even pair is injective is made.

| Observable | Linearized operator / adjoint / exact kernel |
|---|---|
| Bath temperatures and endpoint displacements | Identically zero perturbations: bounded zero operators with zero adjoints; kernel is the whole input space. Prescribed boundary values are not independent observations. |
| Force and two heat powers | Equations (2)–(3) on odd states; on full \(H^2\), use (1) and the bounded Hilbert adjoints of the actual forward maps. The odd graph obstruction is unchanged. |
| Flux sum or difference | On odd states the sum is zero and the difference is \(\mathcal T\); their adjoints/kernels follow from (3). Time-resolved left and right data are duplicate up to sign on the odd sector. |
| Endpoint strain, **additional instrument** | For \(h\in X_o=H^2\cap H_o\), \(\delta u_x=rP+\beta Y\). Since \(P\in H^1_tH^2_x\) and \(Y|_{\partial I}=0\), \(\delta u_x(0,t)=r e^{-\mu t}h(0)\), \(\delta u_x(L,t)=-r e^{-\mu t}h(0)\). The map \(E:X_o\to L^2(J)\) is bounded and rank one; \(E^*f=r\langle f,e^{-\mu t}\rangle R_0\), where \(R_0\in X_o\) is the Riesz representative of the trace \(h\mapsto h(0)\). \(\ker E=\{h:h(0)=0\}\); combined \(\ker(\mathcal T,E)=X_o\cap\ker\mathcal T\cap\ker\operatorname{tr}_0\). This could add one independent functional, but it does not prove that intersection is zero. It is **not** defined as a bounded trace on arbitrary \(H_o\). |

For the sine prior \(V_N\), each \(h(0)=0\), so this strain observable adds **nothing at linear order**. A physical boundary-strain gauge, if feasible without altering clamps or baths, is not implied by a prescribed displacement measurement; finite sensor footprint and calibration must be addressed separately. None of these extra channels removes the full graph obstruction by the calculations above.
