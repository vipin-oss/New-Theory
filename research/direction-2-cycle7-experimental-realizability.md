# Cycle 7: experimental translation, without performance claims

The frozen protocol prescribes both endpoint displacement and bath temperature. The mathematically available target is the signed difference of the two outward heat powers over \(J\), or twice one outward flux on the odd **linearized** sector. A load cell records force, but its linear odd response vanishes. A thermometer at an ideally clamped bath reads the prescribed temperature; an endpoint displacement gauge reads the commanded displacement. Neither supplies another state-sensitive channel in the ideal model. The formulas do not claim either heat-flux sensor is already installed.

A boundary strain gauge could in principle add the one endpoint memory trace derived in the boundary report, if the gauge measures strain at the mathematical endpoint without changing the mechanical or thermal boundary conditions. It is not an \(L^2\)-state observable and vanishes on the chosen sine prior; a finite-size surface gauge measures an average, not the exact trace, and requires a separate forward model. No physical sensor performance, size, thermal contact or response time is asserted.

Finite measurement weights \(e_i\) are precomputed from the **actual calibrated** derivative, geometry, loading and prior. An acquisition system must approximate the continuous weighted integral, after calibration of sign, area and heat-power units. Noise, sampling/quadrature, sensor dynamics, base-parameter error, forward-solver error, initial preparation drift and prior mismatch are distinct error terms; only their independently bounded combined data norm can be inserted into the theorem. No error budget or material values were supplied.

Multiple runs require reproducibly restoring the same microscopic/internal memory profile and initial thermal and mechanical conditions; matching macroscopic force is insufficient to establish that. Different waveforms must remain within independently established solution and positivity regimes. Parameters \(a,b,k,\lambda,c,\kappa,\theta_*,A_0,L\), the actuation and heat transfer law must be independently calibrated or a joint nuisance-parameter problem formulated; otherwise the stated inverse theorem does not apply. The symbolic small-load threshold and coupled-past realizability have not been checked for a real apparatus.
