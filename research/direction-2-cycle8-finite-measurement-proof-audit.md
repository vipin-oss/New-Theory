# Cycle 8: finite measurements, noise and mismatch

Work in the dimensionless convention in the dependency audit. Let \(V_N\) have the \(L^2\) input norm, \(Z=L^2(J)\), and \(L=\mathcal T|_{V_N}\) satisfy \(\|Lv\|_Z\ge c_N\|v\|_2\), \(c_N=\sigma_N/2>0\). Then \(L\) is injective and \(W_N=L(V_N)\) has **exactly** dimension \(N\). Its orthonormal basis \(e_1,\ldots,e_N\) is fixed from the known active base, prior and calibrated model, independently of the unknown \(h\). The functionals are linearly independent on \(W_N\), and their composition with \(L\) has rank \(N\); this is a theoretical statement, not independent physical sensor channels.

Let \(\mathcal N(h)=\mathcal F(h)-\mathcal F(0)\), and choose the convex relative \(H^2\) ball \(B=\{h\in V_N:\|h\|_{H^2}<r_N\}\) within the forward domain, with
\[\sup_{h\in B}\|D\mathcal F(h)|_{V_N}-L\|_{L^2\to Z}\le r:=K_2r_NK_N\le c_N/2.\]
The derivative \(L\) here means the restriction of \(D\mathcal F(0)\). The \(H^2\)-to-\(L^2\) equivalence is applied only to the increment \(v\in V_N\): \(\|v\|_{H^2}\le K_N\|v\|_2\), not to unrestricted states. A straight segment joining two points in \(B\) stays in \(B\). Writing \(P\) for projection onto \(W_N\),
\[\|z(h_1)-z(h_2)\|_{\mathbb R^N}=\|P(\mathcal F(h_1)-\mathcal F(h_2))\|_Z\ge (c_N-r)\|h_1-h_2\|_2\ge \frac{c_N}{2}\|h_1-h_2\|_2.\]
The proof uses the integral of \(D\mathcal F\) along the segment and \(PL=L\). The initial-derivative range projection was already explicitly used in the earlier nonlinear report. This is standard inverse-problem methodology, not a newly discovered general theorem.

Let \(d=z(h)+e\), \(\|e\|_2\le\eta\), and set \(\alpha=c_N-r\ge c_N/2\). An exact solution \(\hat h\in B\) of \(z(\hat h)=d\), **if one exists**, obeys \(\|\hat h-h\|_2\le\eta/\alpha\le2\eta/c_N\). Arbitrary noise need not produce a solution in \(z(B)\); uniqueness only applies inside \(B\).

For least squares choose a nonempty **closed** \(H^2\) sub-ball \(C\subset B\), with \(h\in C\). Finite dimensionality makes \(C\) compact, and continuity of \(z\) gives a minimizer. Since \(h\) is feasible, \(\|z(\hat h)-d\|\le\eta\); thus \(\|\hat h-h\|_2\le2\eta/\alpha\le4\eta/c_N\). An open ball need not attain its infimum; a solver returning only an approximate minimizer requires adding its residual excess to the numerator. Neither existence of a feasible physical state nor an exact numerical minimizer is inferred from the forward theory.

For linear data from \(h=v+r_0\), \(v\in C\subset V_N\), \(r_0\in H_o\), mismatch in measurement space is \(P\mathcal T r_0\), bounded by \(\|\mathcal T\|\|r_0\|_2\). For nonlinear data with \(h\) in the actual strong forward domain, mismatch is \(m=\|P[\mathcal F(h)-\mathcal F(v)]\|\). A local \(H^2\)-derivative bound along the segment yields \(m\le L_{H^2}\|h-v\|_{H^2}\); an \(L^2\)-mismatch bound is not established. If data also contain noise \(\eta\), replace \(\eta\) by \(\eta+m\); this controls distance to \(v\), and distance to the true \(h\) additionally includes \(\|h-v\|_2\). Model/calibration/solver discrepancies require independently quantified data-space bounds and cannot be absorbed without accounting.
