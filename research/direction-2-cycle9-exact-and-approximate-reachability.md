# Cycle 9: exact, approximate and projected reachability

For the selected zero-start, equal-bath, scalar-displacement protocol, the reflection theorem shows \(\operatorname{ran}\mathcal P\subset H_e\) for all terminal-compatible solutions in the unique class, and \(\mathcal P(0)=0\). Therefore
\[\operatorname{ran}\mathcal P\cap V_N=\{0\},\qquad \Pi_{V_N}\operatorname{ran}\mathcal P=\{0\}.\]
This is an exact obstruction, not a statement based on modal truncation. No nonzero odd sine state is reachable, no \(N\)-dimensional odd projection is onto, and there is no approximation of a fixed nonzero odd target in \(L^2\) or \(H^2\). In particular \(L^2\)-approximation would not by itself provide the \(H^2\) smallness required by the nonlinear post-zero theorem, even if it were available; here it is already excluded.

No nontrivial finite-dimensional family of terminal-compatible **full** even states is constructed. Exact \(\theta(\cdot,0)=\theta_*\) is an infinite-dimensional terminal constraint; a compactly supported endpoint displacement does not prove it. The unconstrained forward control-to-state map can be differentiable near zero while the constrained control set has unknown geometry. A finite-dimensional submersion theorem requires a genuinely surjective projected derivative on a specified open domain and valid treatment of all terminal constraints; neither is available here.

**Alternative-family transfer test.** Let \(\Psi:B_\rho^{V_N}\to H^2\) be a genuinely prepared family with \(\Psi(0)=0\), \(\|D\Psi(v)-I\|_{L^2(V_N)\to L^2}\le\varepsilon\), and suppose the full post-zero derivative admits a bounded extension \(L_{\rm full}:L^2(I)\to L^2(J)\) whose restriction to \(H_o\) is \(\mathcal T\), and that all derivative differences used below are legitimately controlled. If \(\mathcal T\) has finite-prior lower bound \(c_N\), then at zero \(\|L_{\rm full} D\Psi(0)v\|\ge[c_N-\|L_{\rm full}\|\varepsilon]\|v\|\); to obtain a nonlinear two-point bound one must additionally control \(D(\mathcal F\circ\Psi)(v)-L_{\rm full} D\Psi(0)\) on a convex parameter ball by a strictly smaller constant, using legitimate strong-topology estimates. This is a **conditional criterion**, not a construction, and the assumed global \(L^2\) extension for nonlinear derivative differences is not available from the existing \(H^2\)-based map.

For the selected symmetric protocol, \(D\Psi(0)v\in H_e\), while \(v\in H_o\); hence \(\|D\Psi(0)v-v\|_2\ge\|v\|_2\), so the desired closeness with \(\varepsilon<1\) is impossible for a nonzero parameter direction. A nearby *odd* finite-prior manifold therefore cannot be obtained merely by renaming these even terminal states. An alternative asymmetric preparation may break the obstruction, but its reachability and transfer are open.
