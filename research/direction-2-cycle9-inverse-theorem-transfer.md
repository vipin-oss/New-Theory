# Cycle 9: consequence for the finite-prior inverse theorem

**Selected protocol: option 5, it cannot prepare nonzero odd memory**, under the stated uniqueness class. The Cycle 8 theorem remains a valid conditional mathematical result on \(V_N\), but the protocol's intersection with that class is \(\{0\}\), so it provides no nontrivial physical recovery problem. Its finite-bin corollary likewise cannot identify a physically prepared odd parameter under this protocol. This does not invalidate either theorem and does not show all other physical preparations fail.

A further output-specific check rules out a cosmetic replacement by the even reachable sector. For any even initial memory in the post-zero unique-solution class, the same reflection argument gives even nonlinear temperature, so its labeled outward flux difference \(\mathcal F(h_e)=A_0\kappa[y_x(0)+y_x(L)]\) is identically zero in \(L^2(J)\). Thus this observation cannot distinguish any two such even states, if two exist. At the derivative level, an even perturbation \(h_e\) gives even \(Y\), so \(Y_x(L)=-Y_x(0)\) and
\[D\mathcal F(0)h_e=A_0\kappa[Y_x(0)+Y_x(L)]=0.\]
If a \(C^1\) finite-dimensional **even** prepared family \(\Psi\) through zero existed, \(D(\mathcal F\circ\Psi)(0)=0\). Hence no bound \(\|\mathcal F(\Psi(a))-\mathcal F(\Psi(0))\|\ge c|a|\), \(c>0\), can hold locally in a nontrivial parameter direction. This exact symmetry result does not rule out identifiability with force or flux sum; those would be different observations and require separate analysis.

A real replacement requires: (i) construct a \(C^1\) terminal-compatible preparation family \(\Psi\) in \(H^2\), with the full initial-temperature and equilibrium constraints; (ii) prove a lower bound on the derivative of the **actual chosen output** composed with \(D\Psi(0)\); (iii) control the nonlinear derivative on a convex parameter neighborhood; and (iv) handle measurement and preparation error in the appropriate norm. For an asymmetric thermal-control alternative, none of (i)–(iv) is proved. Under the selected symmetric protocol, an \(L^2\)-small perturbation of the odd sine prior that would preserve its lower bound is impossible by parity.
