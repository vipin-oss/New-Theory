# Cycle 9: selected preparation protocol and coupled equations

**Selected class.** Fix \(T_p>0\), \(I=(0,L)\), \(s\in[-T_p,0]\), the same material coefficients and baths as the frozen post-zero experiment. At \(s=-T_p\), impose the *fully relaxed, symmetric state* \(u=0,\ z=0,\ \theta=\theta_*>0\). Prescribe \(u(0,s)=0,\ u(L,s)=g_p(s)\), with \(g_p\in C_c^\infty((-T_p,0))\) (one may take its closure in a fixed \(C^1\) or \(H^2\)-in-time norm). Both baths are fixed: \(\theta(0,s)=\theta(L,s)=\theta_*\). There is no body force or internal heat source. Select controls/solutions for which a unique positive solution exists in the stated weak-initial strong-temperature class. The zero control gives an explicit admissible solution, so the class is nonempty; no nonzero control satisfying the terminal thermal constraint is asserted.

At \(s=0\) require \(g_p(0)=0\), \(\theta(\cdot,0)=\theta_*\) and the equilibrium-reconstructed displacement; the latter already gives \(u(0,0)=u(L,0)=0\). Requiring \(u\equiv0\) at the endpoint is **not** the same condition and is not imposed. The terminal temperature is a substantive exact constraint on the control, not an automatic consequence of compact support. The full terminal-state set of such controls need not be an open set or a smooth manifold. The post-zero loading remains exactly the original nonzero sinusoid.

For \(m=g_p/L\), \(D=a+k\), \(r=k/D\), \(\alpha=a/D\), \(\beta=b/D\), \(d=b^2/D\), \(\mu=\lambda\alpha\), \(\gamma=\lambda\beta\), \(y=\theta-\theta_*\), \(v=\bar z\), \(p=Qz\), \(Q=I-\text{mean}\), equilibrium and endpoint conditions give at **every** time
\[\sigma=Dm-b\bar y-kv,\quad u_x=m+rp+\beta Qy,\quad w=u_x-z=m-v-\alpha p+\beta Qy.\]
The full dynamics are
\[v_s=\lambda(m-v),\quad p_s=-\mu p+\gamma Qy,\quad
(cI+d(\theta_*+y)Q)y_s=\kappa y_{xx}-b(\theta_*+y)(m'+rp_s)+k\lambda w^2.\tag{1}\]
Set \((v,p,y)(-T_p)=(0,0,0)\), \(y|_{x=0,L}=0\). Reconstruct \(u(s,x)=\int_0^x[m+rp+\beta Qy]d\xi\); since the mean of \(p,Qy\) is zero, \(u(L,s)=Lm\). Equations (1) retain the original nonlocal mass and both coupling and dissipation. At \(s=0\), \(m=0,y=0\), hence \(u_x=rp\), \(\sigma=-kv\); terminal odd mean-zero memory \(h\) would require \(v=0,p=h\).

A suitable solution class is \(y\in H^1_sL^2_x\cap L^2_s(H^2_x\cap H^1_{0,x})\cap C_sH^1_0\), \(p\in H^1_sL^2_x\) with \(p(0)\in L^2\), \(v\in H^1\), and \(\theta\) bounded above and below by positive constants. Here \(p(-T_p)=0\) and the Volterra formula in (1) imply \(p\in H^1_sH^2_x\), since \(Qy\in L^2_sH^2_x\); thus \(p(0)\in H^2\). This applies to the chosen zero-start strong class, not arbitrary \(L^2\) initial memory. In a fixed nondimensional convention, the prior fixed-point estimates can be adapted to sufficiently small \(\|m\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\) on a fixed horizon, but the original explicit sinusoidal constants are not reused for arbitrary waveforms without recomputation. Our symmetry obstruction requires uniqueness in the chosen admissible class; the zero trajectory and its uniqueness are explicit, while existence for nonzero terminal-compatible loops is not proved.

**One possible alternative, not analyzed as a positive reachability claim:** allow two independently actuated end temperatures during preparation, restored to \(\theta_*\) at both ends and across the whole bar at \(s=0\), while retaining scalar displacement and the frozen post-zero problem. An antisymmetric temperature difference breaks reflection symmetry, but its control well-posedness, positivity and exact terminal constraints must be proved. No distributed source is introduced.
