# Structure of the actual delayed graph operator

All statements are conditional on the active-base, weak and strong estimates audited in `direction-2-active-base-dependency-audit.md`. Under the additional pre-delay threshold, \(G=A_\delta D_\delta^{-1}\). Constants below may depend on the fixed actual base, \(\delta,T,L\), but not on mode index. They are not numerical material estimates.

## 1. Actual high-frequency graph bound

The full-history solution for \(h=\phi_n\) can be written with \(p=P-e^{-\mu t}\phi_n\) and smoothness-level trial
\[U_n(t,x)=\frac{e^{-\mu t}S(t,x)\phi_n(x)}{\kappa q_n^2}.\]
The weak \(V'\)-energy calculation in `direction-2-critical-theorem-independent-audit.md` §§2–3 applies to the actual \(S\in\mathcal A\) and yields, for the error \(e_n=Y_n-U_n\),
\[\|e_n\|_{L^\infty(0,T;L^2)}+\|e_n\|_{L^2(0,T;H^1_0)}\le Cq_n^{-2}.\tag{1}\]
This bound includes the initial mismatch \(-U_n(0)\), the memory error and the \(M U_{n,t}\), \(B U_n\), \(S_x\cos(q_nx)\) terms. In particular, \(S\in C_tH^1_x\) makes \(U_n\in C_tL^2_x\), while the strong solution gives \(Y_n\in C_tL^2_x\); hence the \(L^\infty\) bound holds at the specified \(\delta\). Therefore
\[\|A_\delta\phi_n\|_{L^2}\le Cq_n^{-2}.\tag{2}\]
The inherited energy estimate also gives \(\|A_\delta\phi_n\|_{H^1_0}\le C\). Equation (2) is an upper bound for **actual nonautonomous** columns, not a leading symbol: the error in (1) is the same order as the trial, so its nonzero coefficient cannot be inferred. In particular neither a pseudodifferential principal symbol for \(G\) nor a lower bound is proved.

By Hilbert–Schmidt theory, \(A_\delta:H_o\to H_o\) is Hilbert–Schmidt, since \(\sum q_n^{-4}<\infty\). Moreover, projection \(P_N\) onto the first \(N\) odd sines gives
\[\|A_\delta(I-P_N)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\le C N^{-3/2},\quad s_{N+1}(A_\delta)\le C N^{-3/2}.\tag{3}\]
Consequently \(A_\delta\in\mathcal S_p\) for every \(p>2/3\); no endpoint Schatten claim follows. Composition by bounded \(D_\delta^{-1}\) gives the same singular-value upper bound and Schatten membership for \(G:H_o\to H_o\). This is a compact graph relation generated by the actual PDE, not an arbitrary compact operator.

Interpolation of \(H^1_0\) and \(L^2\) gives \(\|A_\delta\phi_n\|_{H^s}\le Cq_n^{-2(1-s)}\), \(0\le s\le1\). Hence \(A_\delta\), and thus \(G\), are Hilbert–Schmidt \(H_o\to H^s(I)\) for \(0\le s<3/4\). Separately, \(G\) is bounded \(H_o\to H^1_0\) and compact into \(H^s\) for every \(s<1\), by interpolation of compact \(H_o\to L^2\) with bounded \(H_o\to H^1\). No \(H^2\) image, compactness into \(H^1\), nonzero symbol, injectivity, or density of \(\operatorname{ran}G\) follows from these estimates.

## 2. Exact memory smoothing and inverse relation

For any \(t_0>0\), let
\[K_{t_0}h=\gamma\int_0^{t_0}e^{-\mu(t_0-s)}Y[h](s)ds,\qquad D_{t_0}=e^{-\mu t_0}I+K_{t_0}.\]
The strong estimate yields \(\sup_t\|Y(t)\|_{H^1}+\|Y_t\|_{L^2}+\|Y\|_{L^2H^2}\le C\|h\|_2\). Integrating \(\kappa Y_{xx}=MY_t-BY-SP\) against the exponential weight gives an \(L^2\) bound on \((K_{t_0}h)_{xx}\): every term is in \(L^2(Q)\), because \(M,S\) are bounded, \(B\in L^2\), and \(Y\in L^\infty(Q)\). The integral has zero spatial traces, so the 1D Dirichlet elliptic estimate yields
\[K_{t_0}:H_o\to H^2(I)\cap H^1_0(I)\cap H_o\quad\text{bounded}.\tag{4}\]
No integration by parts in time or \(M_t\) is required. Under the audited Neumann condition \(D_\delta^{-1}\) exists and the exact identity
\[D_\delta^{-1}=e^{\mu\delta}I-e^{\mu\delta}K_\delta D_\delta^{-1}\tag{5}\]
shows its deviation from the scaled identity maps \(H_o\) boundedly into \(H^2\cap H^1_0\). This also proves \(D_\delta^{-1}\) preserves the odd \(H^2\) class: \(p\in H^2\cap H_o\) iff \(h=D_\delta^{-1}p\in H^2\cap H_o\). It does not establish that either state has a fully coupled physical past.

The leading term of the inverse is a scaled identity, but composing with (2) does **not** give \(\|G\phi_n\|=O(q_n^{-2})\) without control of high-frequency mixing by \(K_\delta D_\delta^{-1}\); (3) remains valid by singular-value composition.

## 3. Adjoint and range limits

For \(\xi\in V_o'\), \(A_\delta^*\xi\in H_o\) is rigorously defined by \(\langle A_\delta h,\xi\rangle\); equivalently it is the initial-memory component of the backward transposition problem for terminal temperature functional \(\xi\) and zero terminal memory functional. The distributional adjoint has \(-\partial_t(Mz)\), not \(-Mz_t\); terminal/boundary data are encoded by duality, not an asserted classical \(z\) trace. Then \(G^*=(D_\delta^{-1})^*A_\delta^*\), and for future controls \(O^*f=G^*\xi_f+\eta_f\).

These structures reduce the graph equation but neither prove \(\overline{\operatorname{ran}O^*}=H_o\) nor rule out cancellation between \(G^*\xi_f\) and \(\eta_f\). A proven nonzero high-frequency principal symbol and its use in a full signed synthesis would require an \(o(q_n^{-2})\) graph remainder plus control of pre-delay transients and mixing; (1) supplies only \(O(q_n^{-2})\). No such asymptotic is asserted.
