# Actual delayed adjoint and reachable observability

## Scope

This report independently rechecks and makes explicit the transposition construction in `direction-2-odd-delayed-state-adjoint-investigation.md`, rather than claiming its derivation is new. Let \(I=(0,L)\), \(T=10/\lambda\), prescribed \(g(t)=g_*\sin^2(\pi t/T)\), \(\delta=T/100\), \(J=(\delta,T)\). The sign assumptions, exact \(s_*>0\), and fixed-unit convention are as in the active-base report and `direction-2-exact-prior-art-and-overlap-audit.md`, (4). In particular \(0<|g_*|/L<s_*/2\) gives the required positive base. Write
\[
M=c+d\theta_b,\quad B=-dQy_{b,t}-bE_b-br\gamma\theta_b+2k\gamma w_b,\quad S=\lambda\alpha(br\theta_b-2kw_b),
\]
where \(r=k/(a+k)\), \(\alpha=a/(a+k)\), \(\gamma=\lambda b/(a+k)\), \(\mu=\lambda\alpha\), \(d=b^2/(a+k)\). Here \(M\) is even, \(0<m_0\le M\le m_1\), \(M_x\in L^\infty_tL^2_x\), \(B\in L^2(Q_T)\), \(S\in H^1_tL^2_x\cap L^2_tH^2_x\subset L^\infty(Q_T)\), with \(Q_T=(0,T)\times I\). Coefficients are generated by the base; none are independently prescribed physical data.

## 1. Forward weak formulation and duality construction

Put \(H=L^2(I)\), \(H_o=\{h(x)=-h(L-x)\}\), \(V=H^1_0(I)\), \(\mathcal E=H^1_tH\cap L^2_t(H^2\cap V)\). For \(F,G\in L^2(Q_T)\) and \((y_0,p_0)\in V\times H\), the inherited energy proof gives a unique solution
\[
My_t-\kappa y_{xx}=By+Sp+F,\quad p_t+\mu p=\gamma y+G,\quad y|_{\partial I}=0,
\quad y(0)=y_0,\ p(0)=p_0, \tag{1}
\]
with \(y\in\mathcal E\cap C([0,T];V)\), \(p\in H^1_tH\), and their norm bounded by the input norms. The heat equation is an \(L^2(Q_T)\) equality; equivalently for \(v\in V\), a.e. \(t\),
\[
(My_t,v)+\kappa(y_x,v_x)=(By+Sp+F,v),\quad(p_t+\mu p-\gamma y-G,v)=0. \tag{2}
\]
No \(M_t\) appears in this forward estimate. The trace \(y_x(\cdot,0)\in L^2(0,T)\) follows from \(L^2_tH^2_x\). For \(h\in H_o\), take \(y_0=0,p_0=h,F=G=0\); even coefficients preserve oddness and
\[
\mathcal Th=2A_0\kappa y_x(\cdot,0)|_J,\qquad P(t)=e^{-\mu t}h+\gamma\int_0^t e^{-\mu(t-s)}Y(s)ds. \tag{3}
\]
The actual \(H^2\)-admissible odd class is \(X_o=H^2(I)\cap H_o\); the bounded \(H_o\) extension is a linear analytical domain, not a nonlinear admissibility assertion.

For each \(f\in L^2(J)\), extend \(f\) by zero outside \(J\) **only as a boundary control**, and solve (1) with zero initial data for arbitrary full-space \((F,G)\in L^2(Q_T)^2\). The functional
\[
\Lambda_f(F,G)=2A_0\kappa\int_J f(t)y_x(t,0)dt
\]
is bounded by (1) and the trace theorem. Riesz yields a unique \((z_f,w_f)\in L^2(Q_T)^2\) with
\[
\int_{Q_T}(Fz_f+Gw_f)=2A_0\kappa\int_J f(t)y_x(t,0)dt,\quad
\|z_f\|_2+\|w_f\|_2\le C\|f\|_{L^2(J)}. \tag{4}
\]
This is the rigorous adjoint definition, with the *full* interval and all pre-delay propagation; it is not merely an abstract declaration that \(\mathcal T^*\) exists.

To derive the equations, take smooth compactly supported \(u,v\) and use the residuals \(F=Mu_t-\kappa u_{xx}-Bu-Sv\), \(G=v_t+\mu v-\gamma u\) in (4). The pair \((u,v)\) is the zero-initial forward solution for these residuals. Distributionally,
\[
-\partial_t(Mz_f)-\kappa z_{f,xx}-Bz_f-\gamma w_f=0,\qquad -w_{f,t}+\mu w_f-Sz_f=0. \tag{5}
\]
Products are distributions if necessary: \(Bz_f\in L^1\), and the mass stays **inside** the derivative; no pointwise \(M_tz_f\) or boundary trace of \(M_t\) is assumed. To identify the ODE terminal condition, use \(u=0\), smooth \(v(0)=0\) with arbitrary \(v(T)\). From (4), \(w_f\in H^1(0,T;H)\), \(w_f(T)=0\), and
\[
w_f(t)=\int_t^T e^{-\mu(s-t)}S(s)z_f(s)ds\in C([0,T];H). \tag{6}
\]

Formally, Green's identity gives
\[
\kappa\int_0^T[y_x(t,0)z_f(t,0)-y_x(t,L)z_f(t,L)]dt,
\]
so the associated **transposition boundary/terminal data** are
\[
z_f(t,0)=2A_0\mathbf1_J(t)f(t),\quad z_f(t,L)=0,\quad (Mz_f)(T)=0,\quad w_f(T)=0. \tag{7}
\]
For the stated rough coefficients and \(L^2\) boundary control, (7) is understood through (4): \(z_f\) is only known in \(L^2(Q_T)\), and neither its ordinary trace, \(z_{f,x}(0,t)\), nor an ordinary final trace of \(Mz_f\) is asserted. A classical normal derivative for \(z_f\) is **not needed** for the duality: it multiplies the forward Dirichlet value, which is zero. A proof demanding such a trace needs extra assumptions and a separate regularity theorem. The source is zero on \((0,\delta)\) at the boundary, but \(z_f,w_f\) generally propagate backward there and are not zero there.

An equivalent odd representative is the odd part of \(z_f\), with formal boundary values \(A_0\mathbf1_Jf\) and \(-A_0\mathbf1_Jf\): \(Y_x(L)=Y_x(0)\) for odd \(Y\), so the boundary term is exactly \(2A_0\kappa fY_x(0)\). The full representative (7) is simpler for the proof; its memory projection is identical.

For the actual forward \((Y,P)\), put \(v=P-e^{-\mu t}h\). Then \((Y,v)\) solves the zero-initial problem with \(F=Se^{-\mu t}h,G=0\), including all past feedback. By (4), Fubini and (6),
\[
\boxed{\langle\mathcal Th,f\rangle_{L^2(J)}
=\int_{Q_T}e^{-\mu t}h(x)S(t,x)z_f(t,x)dxdt
=\langle h,w_f(0)\rangle_H
=\langle h,\Pi_o w_f(0)\rangle_H.} \tag{8}
\]
Thus \(\mathcal T^*f=\Pi_o w_f(0)\in H_o\), bounded in \(f\). Equation (8) proves the requested weak adjoint identity, including correct initial and terminal orientation. Real spaces are used; in complex spaces take the usual conjugations.

## 2. Exact range reduction and delayed graph

Let \(\mathscr A=\{\Pi_o w_f(0):f\in L^2(J)\}\). Then
\[
\ker(\mathcal T:H_o\to L^2(J))=\mathscr A^\perp,\qquad
\ker\mathcal T=\{0\}\iff\overline{\mathscr A}^{H_o}=H_o. \tag{9}
\]
On the actual smooth class, exact uniqueness is the weaker condition \(X_o\cap\mathscr A^\perp=\{0\}\); \(H_o\)-density is sufficient but need not be necessary. Orthogonality to all \(\Pi_ow_f(0)\) gives precisely the **coupled** constraint: the actual strong forward solution has \(Y_x(0,t)=Y_x(L,t)=0\) a.e. on \(J\), in addition to (1), (3) and homogeneous Dirichlet traces. No further interior or coefficientwise vanishing follows from (9).

Define \(\mathcal S_\delta h=(Y(\delta),P(\delta))\in V_o\times H_o\) and \(\mathcal R_\delta\) as the bounded flux observation of an arbitrary restart pair in that state space. Uniqueness gives
\[
\mathcal T=\mathcal R_\delta\mathcal S_\delta,\quad
\mathcal S_\delta h=\left(Y[h](\delta),e^{-\mu\delta}h+\gamma\int_0^\delta e^{-\mu(\delta-s)}Y[h](s)ds\right). \tag{10}
\]
The graph is \(\mathcal G_\delta=\operatorname{ran}\mathcal S_\delta\), not \(V_o\times H_o\) and not a state with independently chosen \(P(\delta)\). The exact missing assertions are
\[
\ker\mathcal S_\delta=\{0\}\quad\text{and}\quad
\mathcal G_\delta\cap\ker\mathcal R_\delta=\{0\}, \tag{11}
\]
together equivalent to full \(H_o\) injectivity. On \(X_o\), use \(X_o\cap\ker\mathcal S_\delta\) and \(\mathcal S_\delta(X_o)\cap\ker\mathcal R_\delta\). Both parts matter: a zero delayed state can already hide a nonzero initial input. The first condition alone does not settle the second.

The memory component is \(D_\delta=e^{-\mu\delta}I+K_\delta\), with \(K_\delta h=\gamma\int_0^\delta e^{-\mu(\delta-s)}Y[h](s)ds\) bounded into \(H^2\), hence compact on \(H_o\). Thus \(D_\delta\) is Fredholm index zero, but this does **not** make \(\mathcal T\) Fredholm or its kernel finite-dimensional. If \(C_Y\) is a proven bound for \(\|Y_t\|_{L^2(0,\delta;H)}\le C_Y\|h\|_H\), then
\[
\|K_\delta\|\le\tfrac23|\gamma|C_Y\delta^{3/2},\qquad
\tfrac23|\gamma|C_Y\delta^{3/2}<e^{-\mu\delta}\ \Longrightarrow\ D_\delta\text{ invertible and }\ker\mathcal S_\delta=0. \tag{12}
\]
The prior delayed-adjoint report §4 gives a data-only conservative \(C_Y\); (12) is an additional unverified inequality at the **fixed** \(\delta\), not a license to alter the experiment. Even if it holds, reachable delayed boundary uniqueness remains open.

## 3. Exact future targets

The most direct sufficient theorem is construction of controls \(f_{n,j}\in L^2(J)\) with \(\|\Pi_ow_{f_{n,j}}(0)-\phi_n\|_2\to0\) for every odd sine. Their norms may diverge; bounded control norms are not assumed. No such control sequence is presently proved. The companion report obtains a genuine but very narrow nonzero-column prior without asserting this full density theorem.
