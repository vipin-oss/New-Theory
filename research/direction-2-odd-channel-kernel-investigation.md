## 1. Exact setting and operator

Let \(I=(0,L)\), \(J=(\delta,T)\), \(T=10/\lambda\), \(\delta=T/100\), and \(g(t)=g_*\sin^2(\pi t/T)\), \(g_*\ne0\). Use the frozen constants and signs

\[
a,k,\lambda,c,\kappa,L,A_0,\theta_*,M>0,\quad b\ne0,\quad
D=a+k,\quad r=k/D,\quad \alpha=a/D,\quad \beta=b/D,
\quad \mu=\lambda\alpha,\quad d=b^2/D.
\]

Let \(R f(x)=f(L-x)\) and \(X_o=\{h\in H^2(I):Rh=-h\}\); define \(H_o=\{h\in L^2(I):Rh=-h\}\). The assumption \(0<q=|g_*|/L<s_*\), with \(s_*\) exactly (6.3) of `direction-2-formulation-A-existence-investigation.md`, is the previously proved **sufficient** fixed-load condition for the active base, an actual weak-admissible neighborhood and its derivative. Numerical experimental parameters have not been supplied, so this condition is **not verified**. Norms and constants are interpreted in the fixed units of that investigation.

The symmetric active base \((y_b,\theta_b,p_b,w_b,E_b)\) is generated by \(z_0=0\) and the prescribed nonzero loading, with \(\theta_b=\theta_*+y_b\). For \(h\in X_o\), the linearized odd fields \(Y,P\) satisfy

\[
\begin{gathered}
P_t=-\mu P+\lambda\beta Y,\qquad P(\cdot,0)=h,\qquad W=-\alpha P+\beta Y,\\
(c+d\theta_b)Y_t-\kappa Y_{xx}
=-dYQ(y_b)_t-bYE_b-br\theta_bP_t+2k\lambda w_bW,\\
Y(\cdot,0)=0,\qquad Y(0,t)=Y(L,t)=0.\tag{1.1}
\end{gathered}
\]

The notation \(Y(\cdot,0)\) is initial **time**; \(Y(0,t),Y(L,t)\) are spatial boundary values. The odd observation is

\[
\mathcal T_o h=2A_0\kappa\,Y_x(0,\cdot)|_J.\tag{1.2}
\]

This is the measured difference \(Q_0-Q_L\), not a new sensor. Reflection symmetry of the active base and uniqueness of the linearized problem preserve odd parity; odd force variation vanishes. The exact question is \(\mathcal T_o h=0\Rightarrow h=0\) for \(h\in X_o\).

## 2. Equivalent parabolic–memory evolution and function spaces

Let

\[
M_b=c+d\theta_b,\qquad
S=br\mu\theta_b-2k\lambda\alpha w_b
=\lambda\alpha(br\theta_b-2kw_b),
\]
\[
B=-dQ(y_b)_t-bE_b-br\lambda\beta\theta_b+2k\lambda\beta w_b.\tag{2.1}
\]

Substituting \(P_t\) and \(W\) in (1.1), with no approximation, gives

\[
M_bY_t-\kappa Y_{xx}=B Y+S P,\quad
P_t=-\mu P+\lambda\beta Y,\quad Y(\cdot,0)=0,\quad P(\cdot,0)=h.\tag{2.2}
\]

The ODE gives the exact Volterra formula

\[
P(t)=e^{-\mu t}h+\lambda\beta\int_0^t e^{-\mu(t-s)}Y(s)\,ds,\tag{2.3}
\]

so the equivalent scalar formulation is

\[
M_bY_t-\kappa Y_{xx}=BY+S e^{-\mu t}h
+\lambda\beta S(t)\int_0^t e^{-\mu(t-s)}Y(s)\,ds.\tag{2.4}
\]

This is non-autonomous; the coefficient outside the convolution is evaluated at the current \((x,t)\). The initial memory appears as an unknown distributed forcing; it is not a known zero prehistory. Conversely, a solution of (2.4) in the spaces below, together with (2.3), solves (1.1), so the formulations are equivalent.

Under the existing strict smallness condition, \(\theta_*/2\le\theta_b\le3\theta_*/2\). Thus \(M_b\) is positive and bounded. The established solution regularity gives \(y_b\in C_tH^1_0\cap H^1_tL^2_x\cap L^2_tH^2_x\), \(w_b,E_b,\theta_b\in L^\infty_{t,x}\), and \(Q(y_b)_t\in L^2_{t,x}\). Hence \(S\in L^\infty\) and \(B\in L^2_{t,x}\) plus bounded terms; we do **not** claim \(B\in L^\infty\) or time-analytic coefficients. Moreover the active base and \(S\) have continuous \(C_tC_x\) representatives by the one-dimensional embedding and memory formulas.

For \(h\in X_o\), the derivative construction yields a unique odd solution

\[
Y\in\mathcal E_T:=H^1(0,T;L^2(I))\cap L^2(0,T;H^2(I)\cap H^1_0(I)),\quad
P\in H^1(0,T;L^2(I)).\tag{2.5}
\]

The initial values are taken in the time-trace sense, the PDE a.e., and spatial Dirichlet conditions in the trace sense. The estimate from the linearized fixed-point equation and the original linear lemma is

\[
\|Y\|_{\mathcal E_T}+\|P\|_{H^1_tL^2_x}\le C\|h\|_{L^2(I)}.\tag{2.6}
\]

Indeed, the explicit input-dependent terms are bounded by \(C\|h\|_2\) because \(S\) and the base are bounded. The remaining linearized fixed-point operator has norm at most the common contraction constant \(<1\), hence its inverse is bounded. The product \(YQ(y_b)_t\in L^2\) because \(\mathcal E_T\hookrightarrow C_tH^1_x\hookrightarrow L^\infty_{t,x}\). The formula first holds on \(X_o\) and extends uniquely and continuously to \(H_o\) by density; this extension is used only for estimates, not to change the frozen initial topology.

The 1-D endpoint trace theorem gives

\[
\|Y_x(0,\cdot)\|_{L^2(J)}\le C\|Y\|_{L^2_tH^2_x},\quad
\|\mathcal T_o h\|_{L^2(J)}\le C\|h\|_2\le C\|h\|_{H^2}.\tag{2.7}
\]

Thus \(\mathcal T_o:X_o\to L^2(J)\) is bounded. Equality of the observation to zero always means a.e. in \(J\), not pointwise differentiability of the endpoint trace.

## 3. Boundary unique continuation: exact gap

Zero \(Y_x(0,\cdot)\) on \(J\), together with the imposed Dirichlet trace, is lateral Cauchy data for the heat component. In (2.2), however, the source \(SP\) is an unknown distributed field. A scalar homogeneous parabolic unique-continuation theorem does not apply directly to an inhomogeneous equation. As an elementary **illustration only**, an arbitrary smooth compactly supported interior \(U(x,t)\) has zero Dirichlet and Neumann traces while solving a scalar heat equation with source \(U_t-U_{xx}\ne0\); this is **not** claimed to solve (1.1) or provide a kernel element. Here the source is constrained by the ODE, which might restore uniqueness, but that constraint must be included in a proved coupled estimate.

The odd restriction is helpful: because \(QY=Y\), the original rank-one mass becomes multiplication by \(M_b\). Nevertheless \(M_b(x,t)\) varies with the active base, \(B\) contains \(Q(y_b)_t\) known only in \(L^2_{t,x}\), and \(P\) has its own distributed initial state. A boundary Carleman estimate for a coupled parabolic–ODE system would need to absorb or reconstruct \(SP\), justify coefficients at the established regularity, and handle the history already accumulated before \(\delta\). The parabolic–ODE interior-observation literature in §7 does not supply such an estimate for this endpoint observation. We have not proved either

\[
Y_x(0,\cdot)=0\text{ on }J\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
Y=0\text{ on }J\times I,\tag{3.1}
\]

or its failure. The theorem in §6 takes (3.1) as an **additional** assumption; it is not silently inferred from scalar unique continuation.

## 4. Delayed state, and a verifiable conditional nondegeneracy bound

At the exact observation start, continuity of \(P\) in \(L^2\) gives

\[
P(\delta)=e^{-\mu\delta}h+\lambda\beta\int_0^\delta e^{-\mu(\delta-s)}Y(s)\,ds.\tag{4.1}
\]

If \(Y=0\) on \(J\times I\), then \(P(t)=e^{-\mu(t-\delta)}P(\delta)\) on \(J\), and (2.2) implies \(S(t,x)P(t,x)=0\) there. Unless the coefficient is nonzero in the relevant region, this does not immediately imply \(P(\delta)=0\). Even if \(P(\delta)=Y(\delta)=0\), (4.1) contains the pre-observation integral: without backward uniqueness of the **coupled** evolution or another estimate one cannot conclude \(h=0\).

There is a concrete sufficient condition for the nondegeneracy part. With the notation and constants \(W_s,W_R,C_B\) from (5.3), (6.1) of the existence investigation, define

\[
C_w=W_s+2C_BW_R,\qquad q_{\rm nd}=\frac{|b|r\theta_*}{8kC_w}>0.\tag{4.2}
\]

For the active base with \(z_0=0\), its fixed-point estimate gives \(\|y_b\|_{\mathcal E_T}\le2C_Bq\), and (5.2) of that investigation gives \(\|w_b\|_\infty\le C_w q\). If the **additional**, unverified condition

\[
0<q<\min\{s_*,q_{\rm nd}\},\tag{4.3}
\]

holds, then \(\theta_b\ge\theta_*/2\) and

\[
|br\theta_b-2kw_b|\ge |b|r\theta_*/2-2kC_wq
>|b|r\theta_*/4,\qquad
|S|>\lambda\alpha |b|r\theta_*/4.\tag{4.4}
\]

This remains valid for either sign of \(b\). It is a **stronger sufficient condition on the same fixed load**, not an alteration of the physical coefficients or a claim about the actual unspecified value of \(g_*\).

Under (4.3), interior vanishing on \(J\) therefore implies \(P=0\) there and \((Y,P)(\delta)=(0,0)\) in \(L^2\), by time continuity. Equation (4.1) then becomes a cancellation identity, not automatically \(h=0\). A separate coupled backward-uniqueness statement is still needed. None has been proved here.

## 5. Direct observability, compactness, and counterexample check

### 5.1 A proved obstruction to strong memory observability

**Theorem.** Assume only the previously established \(0<q<s_*\). For every positive-length subinterval \(J'\subseteq J\), there is no constant \(C\) such that all odd \(h\in X_o\) satisfy

\[
\|P[h]\|_{L^2(J'\times I)}\le C\|\mathcal T_o h\|_{L^2(J)}.\tag{5.1}
\]

In particular, no estimate controlling the initial \(L^2\) norm uniformly by the frozen \(L^2\) flux trace holds. These statements do not imply a nonzero kernel.

**Proof.** By (2.6), the odd map \(h\mapsto Y[h]\) is bounded from \(H_o\) into \(\mathcal E_T\). For any \(3/2<s<2\), Aubin–Lions gives the compact embedding \(\mathcal E_T\hookrightarrow L^2(0,T;H^s(I))\), and \(H^s(I)\to\mathbb R\), \(v\mapsto v_x(0)\), is continuous. Thus \(\mathcal T_o:H_o\to L^2(J)\) is compact. Also \(h\mapsto Y[h]\) is compact \(H_o\to L^2((0,T)\times I)\); convolution by \(e^{-\mu(t-s)}\) is bounded on that space. Hence (2.3) gives

\[
h\longmapsto P[h]=e^{-\mu t}h+K h,\qquad
K:H_o\to L^2(J'\times I)\text{ compact}.\tag{5.2}
\]

Choose an \(L^2\)-orthonormal odd sequence \(h_n\in X_o\), for instance normalized \(\sin(2n\pi x/L)\). It is weakly zero. Compactness gives \(\mathcal T_o h_n\to0\) and \(Kh_n\to0\), while

\[
\|e^{-\mu t}h_n\|_{L^2(J'\times I)}
=\left(\int_{J'}e^{-2\mu t}\,dt\right)^{1/2}>0.
\]

Thus the left side of (5.1) approaches a strictly positive number and the right side approaches zero, a contradiction. The same sequence rules out \(\|h\|_2\le C\|\mathcal T_o h\|\). □

These are **approximate invisible directions**, not exact invisible inputs: for no individual \(n\) have we proved \(\mathcal T_o h_n=0\). The constant \(C\) in the failed estimate must be uniform over all directions; the conclusion is unaffected by the increasing \(H^2\) norms of the normalized sines. A weaker-norm or conditional a priori estimate is not excluded. A proposed Carleman estimate that bounds the full \(L^2\) memory state by only this fixed endpoint flux on an interior time strip would contradict (5.1); estimates in weaker norms, with source terms, or with different observations are not contradicted.

### 5.2 Adjoint and moments

The extended bounded operator \(\mathcal T_o:H_o\to L^2(J)\) has a Hilbert adjoint, and

\[
\ker\mathcal T_o=\{0\}\ \text{on }H_o
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\overline{\operatorname{ran}\mathcal T_o^*}=H_o.\tag{5.3}
\]

For the actual \(H^2\) question, density of the adjoint range in \(H_o\) is sufficient but stronger than necessary; an \(L^2\)-kernel element need not belong to \(H^2\). A genuine adjoint proof would have to derive boundary transposition for (2.2) with its time-dependent coefficients and show the relevant range density. We do not assert it.

For the odd Dirichlet sine basis \(\phi_n=\sqrt{2/L}\sin(2n\pi x/L)\), define \(\psi_n=\mathcal T_o\phi_n\). Boundedness yields for each \(h=\sum h_n\phi_n\in H_o\),

\[
\mathcal T_o h=\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{n=1}^N h_n\psi_n
\quad\text{in }L^2(J).\tag{5.4}
\]

Injectivity on the frozen space is exactly the unproved \(\ell^2\)-independence of this family for the coefficient sequences representing \(H^2\) odd fields. The basis here is complete in \(H_o\), but finite sine sums are **not** dense in all \(H^2(I)\) with arbitrary endpoint values; (5.4) holds by \(L^2\) convergence and boundedness, without changing the domain. The active multipliers couple the spatial modes, so no scalar modal exponential family or complete moment theorem has been obtained. A uniform Riesz lower bound in the \(L^2\) coefficient norm is impossible by §5.1, though \(\ell^2\)-independence could still hold.

**Counterexample check:** no exact \(h\ne0\) satisfying the complete coupled system and \(\mathcal T_o h=0\) is known from this analysis. Compactness, the common exponential \(e^{-\mu t}\), parity and near-zero outputs do not construct one. The existence of a nonzero exact kernel, the failure of a uniform inverse estimate, approximate invisibility and lack of proof of injectivity are different statements.

## 6. Conditional injectivity theorem with explicit unproved assumptions

**Theorem.** Suppose the frozen model satisfies (4.3) and, in addition, both properties below hold for every odd solution of (1.1) generated by \(h\in X_o\):

- **(UC)** \(Y_x(0,\cdot)=0\) in \(L^2(J)\) implies \(Y=0\) in \(L^2(J\times I)\).
- **(BU)** If \(Y(\delta)=0\) and \(P(\delta)=0\) in \(L^2(I)\), then \(h=0\).

Then \(\ker(\mathcal T_o:X_o\to L^2(J))=\{0\}\).

**Proof.** Zero observation implies zero Neumann trace because \(A_0\kappa>0\). By (UC), \(Y=0\) on \(J\times I\), hence \(Y_t=Y_{xx}=0\) there in the weak sense. Equation (2.2) then gives \(SP=0\). By (4.4), \(P=0\) on \(J\times I\). Since \(Y,P\in C([0,T];L^2(I))\), their values at \(\delta\) are zero. Apply (BU) to obtain \(h=0\). □

(UC) is an unproved boundary-to-interior statement for the **coupled system**, and (BU) is an unproved backward uniqueness statement for its **two-component state**, not for a scalar homogeneous heat equation. Both are additional assumptions, not conclusions of the strict fixed-point bound. They are sufficient, not claimed necessary. The theorem does not establish them from the frozen model, nor does it assert a Lipschitz estimate. In view of §5.1, any proof of (UC) must not inadvertently claim an impossible full-memory \(L^2\) estimate of the form (5.1).

## 8. Remaining mathematical work and final decision

A successful proof could establish (UC) and (BU) in §6 under the actual base regularity, or bypass them with a rigorous adjoint-density or complete-moment argument. Alternatively an exact nonzero \(h\in X_o\) must be verified in (1.1) and have zero endpoint flux on all of \(J\). The explicit nondegeneracy condition (4.3) addresses one coupling obstacle but not either continuation property; it is unverified for the prescribed numerical experiment. No numerical or heuristic result is treated as a proof, and no nonlinear conclusion follows.

**Final verdict: CONDITIONAL RESULT.** Injectivity follows from the separately stated, currently unproved (UC) and (BU) assumptions together with (4.3). Under the original strict smallness assumption alone, the odd kernel is **not determined**; §5 proves approximate invisibility and failure of a strong memory-state observability estimate, not an exact nonzero kernel.
