## 3. Explicit quotient representation and finite-dimensional compression

### 3.1 Exact trajectories on the delayed interval

For \(p\in P_*\), let \(h_1=D_1^{-1}p\in H_1\). Its graph-reachable delayed state is
\[
A_*p=Y[h_1](\delta),\qquad p=P[h_1](\delta)
=e^{-\mu\delta}h_1+\gamma\int_0^\delta e^{-\mu(\delta-r)}Y[h_1](r)dr.\tag{6}
\]
For \(t\in J\), its continuation satisfies
\[
P(t)=e^{-\mu(t-\delta)}p+\gamma\int_\delta^t e^{-\mu(t-r)}Y(r)dr,\quad Y(\delta)=A_*p,\tag{7}
\]
\[
M_bY_t-\kappa Y_{xx}-BY
=S(t)e^{-\mu(t-\delta)}p+\gamma S(t)\int_\delta^t e^{-\mu(t-r)}Y(r)dr.\tag{8}
\]
The initial temperature \(A_*p\) and \(p\) contain the complete pre-delay evolution through (6); neither is prescribed independently in this graph. Equations (7)–(8) are an exact causal Volterra reduction in time, not a diagonal spectral model. The endpoint derivative is the \(L^2(J)\) trace of the strong-temperature solution, not a pointwise boundary evaluation of (8).

For a more explicit operator formula, let \(\mathcal H(y_0,F)\) be the unique solution on \(J\) of
\[
M_by_t-\kappa y_{xx}-By=F,\quad y(\delta)=y_0,\quad y|_{\partial I}=0.
\]
The energy argument in the preceding report with \(S=0\) gives a bounded map \(V\times L^2(J\times I)\to\mathcal E_J\), where \(\mathcal E_J=H^1_tH\cap L^2_t(H^2\cap H^1_0)\), with its continuous \(V\) time trace. Define bounded causal operators
\[
(\mathcal V y)(t)=\mathcal H\!\left(0,\gamma S(t)\int_\delta^t e^{-\mu(t-r)}y(r)dr\right)(t),
\quad \mathcal Gp=\mathcal H(A_*p,S e^{-\mu(t-\delta)}p).
\tag{9}
\]
The integral is forward-oriented and \(S\) is evaluated at the current time. The coupled restart well-posedness shows \(I-\mathcal V\) is invertible on \(\mathcal E_J\): for any \(g\in\mathcal E_J\), solve the coupled problem with initial temperature \(g(\delta)\), zero initial memory and heat forcing \(M_bg_t-\kappa g_{xx}-Bg\in L^2\). Its temperature \(y\) satisfies \(y-\mathcal V y=g\); uniqueness and the energy bound give a bounded inverse. Consequently
\[
\Gamma p=\pi\,2A_0\kappa\,\operatorname{tr}_{x=0}\partial_x
\left[(I-\mathcal V)^{-1}\mathcal Gp\right],\tag{10}
\]
where \(\pi:Y_{\rm out}\to Y_{\rm out}/O_*\). This retains all actual \(M_b,B,S\), the memory feedback, the true delayed temperature, and the endpoint trace. It is not a proof of a scalar backward-uniqueness theorem. No simpler operator with a known exact kernel has been identified; compactness by itself makes writing a difference of compact operators a tautology, not a useful perturbation theorem.

### 3.2 New exact compression theorem

Identify \(Y_{\rm out}/O_*\) isometrically with \(O_*^\perp\) using \(Q_O=I-P_{O_*}\). Since for \(e\in E\), \(\mathcal S e=(Ue,0)\),
\[
O_*=\mathcal T(E),\qquad
\widehat\Gamma:=Q_O\mathcal T|_{H_1}D_1^{-1}:P_*\to O_*^\perp.\tag{11}
\]
The quotient operator \(\Gamma\) is identified with \(\widehat\Gamma\). Thus the quotient removes exactly the finite-dimensional set of output functions \(\mathcal T(E)\), no more. It **can** create additional kernel vectors in the graph component: \(p\ne0\) is killed precisely when its actual output is equal to an output from some \(e\in E\). This does not mean the corresponding original graph input alone had zero output; the correcting vertical state is essential.

**Theorem 1 (exact input-kernel reduction).** With the above frozen operators,
\[
\boxed{\ker\widehat\Gamma=D_1\bigl(\Pi_{H_1}\ker\mathcal T\bigr),\qquad
\ker(\mathcal T|_E)=\{e\in E:Ue\in F_0\}.}\tag{12}
\]
The map \(e\mapsto Ue\) maps \(\ker(\mathcal T|_E)\) onto \(F_0\) with kernel \(N\). In particular there are bounded Hilbert-space isomorphisms
\[
\ker\widehat\Gamma\cong\ker\mathcal T/\ker(\mathcal T|_E),\qquad
F_0\cong\ker(\mathcal T|_E)/N.\tag{13}
\]
All statements in (12)–(13) are on the \(H\)-extension; the quotient by a closed finite-dimensional subspace carries its Hilbert quotient norm.

**Proof.** If \(p\in\ker\widehat\Gamma\), put \(h_1=D_1^{-1}p\). Then \(\mathcal T h_1\in O_*=\mathcal T(E)\), so some \(e\in E\) satisfies \(\mathcal T(h_1+e)=0\). Therefore \(p=D_1\Pi_{H_1}(h_1+e)\). Conversely, if \(h=h_1+e\in\ker\mathcal T\), then \(\mathcal T h_1=-\mathcal T e\in O_*\), so \(D_1h_1\in\ker\widehat\Gamma\). The map \(h\mapsto D_1\Pi_{H_1}h\) is bounded and onto \(\ker\widehat\Gamma\); its kernel on \(\ker\mathcal T\) is exactly \(\ker(\mathcal T|_E)\). The induced bounded bijection between Hilbert quotients has bounded inverse by the open mapping theorem. Finally \(\mathcal T e=\mathcal R(Ue,0)\), \(N=E\cap\ker U\), and \(U(E)=F_*\); this gives the second isomorphism. □

Consequences are exact, with dimensions possibly infinite:
\[
\dim\ker\mathcal T=\dim N+\dim F_0+\dim\ker\Gamma.\tag{14}
\]
Hence \(\ker\Gamma\) is finite-dimensional **if and only if** \(\ker(\mathcal T:H\to Y_{\rm out})\) is finite-dimensional. In particular, quotienting has not secretly converted the full problem into a finite-dimensional one. If \(\ker\Gamma=0\), then \(\ker\mathcal T\subset E\subset X\); in that special case injectivity on \(X\) and \(H\) are equivalent, but it is **not** known that \(\ker\Gamma=0\). More generally, since \(E\subset X\), an injectivity proof restricted to \(X\) would already force \(N=F_0=0\); it need not eliminate \(\ker\Gamma\) vectors whose associated original input is not in \(X\).

Theorem 1 is a further exact relation and a diagnostic of what the quotient does. Its equations also show its limitation: proving \(\ker\Gamma=0\) remains equivalent to excluding all full observation-kernel directions apart from the finite-dimensional \(E\) component; no such exclusion follows from (12) alone.

## 4. Quotient elimination, continuation, and compactness

If \(\Gamma p=0\), equation (5) supplies a \(v_p\in F_*\) with
\[
\mathcal R(A_*p+v_p,p)=0\quad\text{throughout }J\text{ in }L^2(J).\tag{15}
\]
This is exact for all observation times in the a.e. sense, not a finite sample. Because \(v_p\in U(E)\), the corrected pair is reachable from an actual \(h_1+e\), and its two endpoint derivatives vanish by oddness. No result here proves that this condition forces \(p=0\). The \(O_*\) outputs are the span of finitely many actual functions \(\mathcal T e_i\); finite-dimensionality does not imply these time functions are analytic or satisfy a closed finite-dimensional ODE. Such properties have not been established for the time-dependent active coefficients.

If a separate coupled continuation result proved \(Y=0\) for the corrected trajectory, the heat equation gives \(SP=0\). The previously documented **additional**, unverified sufficient condition \(q<q_{\rm nd}\) supplies \(|S|\ge s_0>0\), so \(P=0\) on \(J\), the delayed pair is zero, and in particular \(p=0\). This conditional chain does not prove the missing continuation or \(N=0\); it also is not the only conceivable path to \(\ker\Gamma=0\). Formula (8) has an exponentially smooth temporal kernel, but its multiplier \(S(t,x)\), the distributed initial source, and the nonautonomous rough coefficients remain; no applicable coupled backward-uniqueness theorem has been established from these facts. A scalar homogeneous heat backward theorem alone cannot be substituted.

Since \(P_*\) is finite-codimensional in infinite-dimensional \(H\), it is infinite-dimensional. Compactness of \(\Gamma\) gives, for any orthonormal \((p_n)\subset P_*\),
\[
\|\Gamma p_n\|\longrightarrow0.\tag{16}
\]
It forbids a uniform estimate \(\|p\|_H\le C\|\Gamma p\|\) for every \(p\in P_*\). It does not determine \(\ker\Gamma\). For instance the compact operators on \(\ell^2\), \(x\mapsto(x_n/n)\) and \(x\mapsto(0,x_2/2,x_3/3,\ldots)\), respectively have zero and nonzero kernels and both have approximate invisible unit directions. These are functional-analysis illustrations, **not** counterexamples to the PDE. No uniform observability or exact non-injectivity follows from (16).

## 5. Quotient adjoint, density, and the vertical Gram test

### 5.1 Exact quotient adjoint

Let \(J_A:P_*\to Z_1\), \(J_Ap=(A_*p,p)\), and let \(j:O_*^\perp\hookrightarrow Y_{\rm out}\) be inclusion. Then
\[
\widehat\Gamma=j^*\mathcal R J_A,\qquad
\widehat\Gamma^*=J_A^*\mathcal R^*j.\tag{17}
\]
Here \(\mathcal R^*:Y_{\rm out}\to Z_1\) is the Hilbert adjoint for the chosen \(V\times H\) product inner product. If \(\mathcal R^*f=(a_f,b_f)\), then
\[
\widehat\Gamma^* f=A_*^*a_f+\Pi_{P_*}b_f,\qquad f\in O_*^\perp.\tag{18}
\]
Equivalently, from (11),
\[
\boxed{\widehat\Gamma^* f=(D_1^{-1})^*\Pi_{H_1}\mathcal T^*f
=(D_1^{-1})^*\Pi_{H_1}\Pi_o w_f(0),\quad f\in O_*^\perp.}\tag{19}
\]
Here \((D_1^{-1})^*:H_1\to P_*\) is a bounded bijection, and \(\mathcal T^*f=\Pi_o w_f(0)\) is the previously justified coupled transposition adjoint. These are not asserted to be classical boundary traces.

The exact density criterion is
\[
\ker\Gamma=\{0\}\iff
\overline{\operatorname{ran}\widehat\Gamma^*}^{\,P_*}=P_*
\iff
\overline{\{\Pi_{H_1}\Pi_o w_f(0):f\in O_*^\perp\}}^{\,H_1}=H_1.
\tag{20}
\]
The finite-dimensional quotient does not automatically make density easier: it also restricts the admissible controls to \(O_*^\perp\). Equations (19)–(20) supply the exact missing theorem: for every nonzero \(h_1\in H_1\), find an \(f\perp\mathcal T(E)\) whose coupled adjoint satisfies \(\langle h_1,\Pi_o w_f(0)\rangle\ne0\). No such separating construction is known here. A dense range of a compact adjoint is possible and does not furnish a uniform lower bound.

Since \(f\perp O_*=\mathcal T(E)\) iff \(\mathcal T^*f\perp E\), the projected term in (19) can also be written simply as \(\mathcal T^*f\in H_1\). Thus the control restriction is not an invented boundary condition: it exactly annihilates all outputs from the delayed-memory kernel \(E\).

### 5.2 The finite-dimensional vertical part

Choose any orthonormal basis \(v_1,\ldots,v_m\) of \(F_*\), and set \(o_i=\mathcal R(v_i,0)\in L^2(J)\). Define the symmetric Gram matrix
\[
G_{ij}=\langle o_i,o_j\rangle_{L^2(J)}.\tag{21}
\]
Then
\[
F_0=\left\{\sum_i c_iv_i:Gc=0\right\},\qquad
F_0=\{0\}\iff G\text{ is positive definite}\quad(m=0\text{ included}).\tag{22}
\]
Indeed \(c^TGc=\|\sum c_io_i\|^2\). This is an exact finite-dimensional criterion using the **entire** interval \(J\), not a proof that its determinant is nonzero. The matrix entries depend on the actual active coupled evolution; no numerical coefficient data are available and no rank is asserted. Since \(F_*=U(E)\), choose \(e_i\in E\) with \(Ue_i=v_i\); then \(o_i=\mathcal T e_i\). Every \(e_i\) is in \(H^2\cap H^1_0\cap H\).

If \(v\in F_0\setminus\{0\}\), choose \(e\in E\) with \(Ue=v\). Then \(e\ne0\), \(e\in X\), and \(\mathcal T e=\mathcal R(v,0)=0\): it is an exact invisible **linearized** odd input. No such \(v\) has been shown to exist. Conversely, \(N\ne0\) alone is already an exact invisible input in \(X\), although it gives \(v=0\). Therefore the vertical part can in principle be checked by (22) without resolving \(\ker\Gamma\), but its zero status is unproved; the conditional Neumann bound \(E=0\) would remove it and is not verified for the experiment.

## 6. Coupled adjoint through the unobserved interval

The actual adjoint is constructed by transposition from the forward source problem. Its distributed equations are
\[
-\partial_t(M_bz)-\kappa z_{xx}-Bz-\gamma w=0,\qquad
-w_t+\mu w-Sz=0.\tag{23}
\]
For \(f\in L^2(J)\), \(z\in L^2_{t,x}\), \(w\in H^1_tL^2_x\), \(w(T)=0\); the heat boundary input is \(z(t,0)=2A_0\mathbf1_J(t)f(t)\), \(z(t,L)=0\), and \(M_b(T)z(T)=0\) **in the transposition sense**. No classical trace of \(z\) is claimed. The distributional ODE gives, on \(0\le t\le\delta\),
\[
w(t)=e^{-\mu(\delta-t)}w(\delta)
+\int_t^\delta e^{-\mu(s-t)}S(s)z(s)ds.\tag{24}
\]
The boundary input is zero there, but neither \(z\) nor \(w\) is thereby zero: they carry data generated by the later controlled interval. Equation (24) is valid in \(L^2(I)\) and does not by itself prove density of \(\Pi_ow(0)\).

The factorization \(\mathcal T^*=\mathcal S^*\mathcal R^*\) clarifies pre-observation density. **If** the full set \(\operatorname{ran}\mathcal R^*\) were dense in \(Z_1\), continuity of \(\mathcal S^*\) would give
\[
\overline{\operatorname{ran}\mathcal T^*}^H
=\overline{\operatorname{ran}\mathcal S^*}^H
=(\ker\mathcal S)^\perp=N^\perp.\tag{25}
\]
Thus pre-delay evolution can destroy full density exactly when \(N\ne0\), under that **unproved full-state density hypothesis**. Density of only a delayed-memory component is not enough: the adjoint temperature contribution \(U^*a_f\) may correlate or cancel with \(D_\delta^*b_f\). This argument does not assert \(\mathcal R^*\) is dense. The quotient's finite-dimensional obstruction is precisely the restriction \(f\perp\mathcal T(E)\) in (19); it is not an assertion that the rest of the uncontrolled kernel is finite-dimensional.

A sufficient coupled unique-continuation/separation theorem, stated without assuming its conclusion, would need to establish that every nonzero \(h_1\in H_1\) produces a graph-reachable trajectory whose full delayed output is **not** an element of \(\mathcal T(E)\). Equivalently, it could construct controls satisfying the separation in (20). It must include the variable-coefficient parabolic–distributed-ODE dynamics, the delayed initial graph relation, and only the given endpoint measurement. Neither the scalar heat theorem nor the formal adjoint alone supplies it. The equivalence to (20) is a target specification, not an independent proof of density.

## 7. Audit of the abstract delayed-memory example

The previous report's example takes \(M_b=\kappa=1\), \(\rho_1=(2\pi/L)^2\), \(\mu>0\), \(\gamma\ne0\), \(\omega=\pi/(2\delta)\), \(B=\rho_1-\mu\), \(S=-\omega^2/\gamma\), and
\[
P=e^{-\mu t}\cos(\omega t)\phi_1,\qquad
Y=-\frac\omega\gamma e^{-\mu t}\sin(\omega t)\phi_1,
\quad\phi_1=\sqrt{2/L}\sin(2\pi x/L).\tag{26}
\]
Differentiation gives \(P_t+\mu P=\gamma Y\) and
\[
Y_t+\rho_1Y=(\rho_1-\mu)Y-\frac{\omega^2}{\gamma}P=BY+SP.
\]
All coefficients are smooth, symmetric, bounded and \(M_b>0\); \(Y(0)=0\), \(P(0)=\phi_1\), and \(Y\) has the required spatial Dirichlet traces. At \(\delta\), \(P=0\) but \(Y=-(\omega/\gamma)e^{-\mu\delta}\phi_1\ne0\). Thus this example proves only that **abstract** \(D_\delta\) can have a nonzero kernel; it does not show \(N\ne0\) or \(\ker\Gamma\ne0\), and the flux is not zero on an entire delayed interval.

It cannot satisfy the actual constitutive relations with the frozen zero-memory base. Indeed the real base has \(m(0)=0\), \(y_b(0)=0\), zero base memory, hence \(w_b(0)=0\), \(\theta_b(0)=\theta_*\). By (1),
\[
S(0)\gamma=\lambda^2\alpha r\theta_*\frac{b^2}{a+k}>0,\tag{27}
\]
whereas the example has \(S\gamma=-\omega^2<0\). The base and \(S\) have continuous representatives, so the conflict is not an arbitrary choice of a single measure-zero coefficient value. The example also prescribes \(M_b,B,S\) independently rather than deriving them from the active base. It cannot be embedded as stated in the physical experiment.

No corresponding abstract example with a nonzero **joint** delayed-state kernel has been constructed. For the particular constant-coefficient example (26), each sine mode evolves by a finite-dimensional matrix exponential and its joint state cannot become zero at a finite time from a nonzero initial mode; this observation does not establish backward uniqueness for all variable-coefficient systems in the stated class. No embedding of a joint-kernel example in the actual base equations is shown.
