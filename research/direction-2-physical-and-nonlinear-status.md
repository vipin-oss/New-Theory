# Physical past and nonlinear status

**Physical past admissibility: OPEN. Nonlinear infinite-dimensional uniqueness: OPEN.**

The linear analytical domain \(H_o=L^2_o(I)\) allows arbitrary odd memory and is not a physical history theorem. For \(X_o=H^2(I)\cap H_o\), the linear uniqueness question is \(X_o\cap\ker\mathcal T=\{0\}\); it can be true even if the full \(H_o\) kernel is nonzero. Under the audited isomorphism, \(D_\delta\) and \(D_\delta^{-1}\) preserve \(X_o\), since their deviation from a scalar identity maps \(H_o\) into \(H^2\). The third class—states generated by a fully coupled admissible thermomechanical past—requires the strain, spatially constant stress, bath temperature, heat equation, memory ODE, load history and initial/topological restrictions to hold together. A scalar constitutive ODE construction alone does not prove this. No nonzero actual invisible state or coupled physical past realizing one is proposed.

In the inherited strict small-load weak-initial regime, the nonlinear observation map is \(C^2\) on a small \(H^2\) initial-data neighborhood, with derivative \(\mathcal T\) on odd perturbations and a quadratic remainder in the \(H^2\) norm. This uses the contraction/implicit-function and strong-trace hypotheses, not an experimental validation. Even an injective derivative would not alone imply nonlinear local injectivity in infinite dimensions: no bounded inverse has been obtained. On the open odd prior, the derivative is compact as an \(H_o\to L^2(J)\) map; the full force/two-flux derivative is its fixed finite vector multiple. It cannot be bounded below on infinite-dimensional \(H_o\). Differentiating a hypothetical local Lipschitz inverse inequality along all smooth odd directions would produce such a lower bound, contradicting compactness; the analogous \(H^2\)-norm statement follows because the derivative \(H^2_o\to Z\) is compact. This is an obstruction to that Lipschitz stability, **not** nonlinear noninjectivity, and says nothing by itself about logarithmic or compact-prior moduli.

The conditional endpoint regularity concerns singular-value summability, not graph uniqueness or a nonlinear inverse theorem. Separate proofs are required for each prior and for any actual coupled past.
